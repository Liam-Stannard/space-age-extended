# Sinks and dead ends

Where the Core's line terminates, and where it can stall. Written while drawing
the full production tree, because a diagram makes an item with nowhere to go
obvious in a way a recipe file does not.

**Nothing here is a bug report about a broken mod.** Every product in the mod
today has somewhere to go. What this document records is the *shape* of those
sinks — how many, whether they are ongoing or one-time, and which of them can
back up and stop the line — because three of them are load-bearing on numbers
that have never been played, and two more only become problems if
`06-core-production-tree.md` is implemented as drafted.

Read §2 and §4 before implementing, §2 and §6 during balancing.

---

## How this was checked

Every `type = "recipe"` declaration in `prototypes/` was parsed by brace
matching, its `ingredients` and `results` collected, and each product's
consumers counted. Sinks that are not recipes were collected separately:
`place_result`, `plant_result`, `place_as_tile`, `fuel_category`, the silo's
`rocket_parts_required`, and research consuming a `tool`.

Two limits on that, both worth closing:

- **It reads source, not a dump.** `tools/check-data-stage.sh` needs a Factorio
  binary and was not available. The source declares 36 recipes — 33 written out
  plus three generated by the stub loop at `prototypes/core/endgame.lua:38` —
  where `05-core-remaining.md` reports 39 from a data-raw dump. That gap is
  unexplained and a dump-based re-run should settle it.
- **It does not model rates.** Everything in §2 about stalling is arithmetic on
  recipe ratios, not something observed on a running line.

## 1. Nothing is orphaned today

Three products are consumed by no recipe at all. All three are consumed by
something that is not a recipe, so none is a defect:

| Product | What consumes it |
| ------- | ---------------- |
| Field Coil Segment | the array's `rocket_parts_required = 100` (`endgame.lua:186`), not a recipe |
| Geodynamic science pack | research; it is a `tool` |
| Seed missile | it is `type = "ammo"` — it gets fired |

Every recipe is unlocked by some technology, so nothing is stranded behind a
missing unlock either. The four capstone stubs are `enabled = true` rather than
tech-gated, which is deliberate: the endgame has to be reachable for playtesting
before the four trees exist.

## 2. The real dead ends are byproducts, and they sit at the root

An output that cannot leave stops the recipe that made it — a full fluid box or
a full item slot both block a machine. Two of the Core's outputs are
unconditional, and both come out of settling, which is the root of everything.

### 2.1 Steam is the one that can stop the planet

Both settling recipes emit steam every craft and there is no way to refuse it:

| Recipe | Per 16 s craft | Steam per second |
| ------ | -------------- | ---------------- |
| `sae-gravity-settling` (`recipes.lua:32`) | 60 melt + 2 dross + 150 steam | 9.4 |
| `sae-quenched-settling` (`recipes.lua:53`) | 25 melt + 2 dross + 900 steam | 56 |

Nothing in the mod consumes steam. Its only sink is a vanilla steam turbine,
which takes 60 a second — and which stops drawing the moment the grid is
satisfied. So a well-powered Core, with arc masts and radiant generators already
covering demand, backs steam up into the settling vessels and stalls the line
that feeds the entire tree.

This is the sharpest edge in the mod, and it is entirely a balance question:
the more successful the player's other power, the more likely it is to bite.
Options, none of them decided:

- leave it, and treat "your power is too good" as a real and legible problem the
  player solves by settling less or venting to turbines they let run;
- give the Core a steam sink that is not power — a condenser returning water
  would be wrong on a world with no water, but a heat sink that voids steam for
  a modest energy cost would work;
- add a third settling recipe that emits no steam at a much worse melt yield, so
  the choice includes "neither".

The first is the most interesting and the most likely to read as a bug to a
player who does not know why their foundries stopped.

### 2.2 Dross has the same shape, and the arithmetic saves it

Two dross per settle, always, into an item slot that blocks when full. Its two
sinks:

- **Whisker beds** (`recipes.lua:90`) — 4 dross + 1 plate for 4 tiles. A
  one-time construction cost; it will not absorb a running line.
- **Dross resettling** (`recipes.lua:76`) — 10 dross + 20 molten kamacite → 20
  settled melt, in 12 s.

So resettling is the only steady-state sink, and it consumes 0.83 dross a second
against the 0.125 a second one settling vessel makes — roughly one resettler per
seven vessels, at a cost of 4% more molten kamacite. That works comfortably. The
thing to notice is that it is **mandatory rather than optional**: a player who
reads resettling as an efficiency nicety and skips it stalls settling. Worth a
line in the technology description, or an in-game hint.

## 3. Single-sink chains

Not broken, but with no slack — if the one consumer is unbuildable, everything
upstream of it is dead:

| Product | Only consumer | What that consumer needs |
| ------- | ------------- | ------------------------ |
| Cast ingot | `sae-orbital-homogenisation` (`recipes.lua:115`) | `gravity max 0` — a platform over the Core |
| Homogenised ingot | `sae-field-conductor` (`intermediates.lua:62`) | the Fulgora ↔ Aquilo capstone |
| Insulation sleeve | `sae-coil-assembly` | — |
| Magnetic core billet | `sae-coil-assembly` | — |
| Coolant charge | `sae-coolant-loop` | — |

The first two matter most, and they compound: the surface metal chain ends in a
recipe that only runs in orbit, whose product ends in a recipe that only runs
with an import. Both are deliberate — that is what "the crossing cannot be
dodged" means — but it does mean a player who lands on the Core with no platform
and no F ↔ A tree can build the whole smelting line and have it terminate in a
chest.

## 4. Two dead ends that `06-core-production-tree.md` would create

Both are cheap to avoid at implementation time and expensive to notice later.

### 4.1 Welded plate loses its only ongoing sink

Today cold welding survives because the coil assembly consumes 2 welded plates
(`endgame.lua:134`), which puts it on the critical path of every segment. The
draft's T9 coil assembly is winding pack + magnetic core billet + reinforced
frame + vacuum cell — **welded plate is not in it.** Its remaining consumers are
arc masts, the sealed roboport and the array itself: all one-time constructions.

After the player has built those, cold welding has nothing left to do. That is a
poor outcome for one of the Core's four signature mechanics, and it happens
silently, as a consequence of a list that simply does not mention it.

**Before implementing T8–T9:** either keep welded plate in the coil assembly, or
route it into the vacuum cell — which is where it arguably belongs, since
vacuum-sealing and cold-welding are the same physical advantage and the draft
names vacuum as the tier's anchor.

### 4.2 Kamacite fines repeat the steam problem one tier up

T1 crushing produces fines as tailings, and T3's metal carbonyl is their only
sink. The draft also routes kamacite plate through crushed kamacite, so
beneficiation stops being optional.

Put together: a player who builds T1 before T3 backs fines up, stalls the
crusher, and loses plate — the whole line, from the tier that now gates it. The
draft's own build order (T1–T2 first, T3 second) walks straight into this.

**Before implementing T1:** give fines a second sink that exists at T1 — the
obvious one is that fines can be smelted back into plate at a worse ratio than
crushed kamacite, which is true to what tailings are and makes the beneficiation
choice a real one rather than a formality. Otherwise reorder the build so T3
lands with T1.

## 5. Terminals, correctly so

For completeness, the things that end the line on purpose and need no action:

- **The Ignition Array** — the point of the mod.
- **Geodynamic science pack** — consumed by research; §6 of `04-the-core.md`
  wants this competing with construction, and it does.
- **The six placeable buildings**, the whisker bed tile, the seed plate
  (planted), the seed missile (fired).
- **Radiant fuel** — burned in the radiant generator, its own fuel category,
  nothing else takes it.

One structural note: the **radiant chunk → radiant fuel → radiant generator**
branch never rejoins the field coil line. It is power, not material, so that is
correct — but it does mean the corridor's seeding mechanic produces nothing the
array needs, and if seeding ever feels like a detour, this is why.

## 6. The checklist

Before implementing:

- [ ] **T1** — fines have a sink that exists at T1, or T3 lands at the same time
      (§4.2).
- [ ] **T8–T9** — welded plate stays on the segment's critical path (§4.1).
- [ ] Any new recipe with an unconditional byproduct gets a sink built in the
      same phase, not a later one.
- [ ] Any new product with exactly one consumer is a deliberate choice, and the
      consumer is not gated behind an unbuilt tree.

During balancing:

- [ ] **Steam.** Decide whether an over-powered Core stalling settling is a
      feature or a defect, and if a defect, which of §2.1's three fixes (§2.1).
- [ ] **Dross.** Confirm one resettler per seven vessels holds on a real line,
      and make resettling's necessity legible rather than discoverable by
      stalling (§2.2).
- [ ] **Cast ingot and homogenised ingot.** Check what a player who lands with
      neither a platform nor F ↔ A actually experiences (§3).
- [ ] Re-run the sink analysis against a real data-raw dump, and resolve the
      36-vs-39 recipe count while doing it (§How this was checked).

# Option B — the Trommel

**One line.** A long screen drum lying across the footprint in a roller cradle, turned by a ring gear at one end.

## The idea

Stop shaking the machine and turn it instead. A trommel is a cylindrical
screen lying almost horizontal: material tumbles down its length, the fine stuff
falls through the mesh into the shell under it and the coarse stuff walks out the
far end. It is the oldest sorting machine there is, and it is a *cylinder* -- a
silhouette nothing else on the Core has.

The drum is inside a half-shell housing, so what shows is the drum's two end
plates, the ring gear and the roller cradle it rides on.

## Why it suits this machine

It sorts by size and by fall, which is what the recipe says, and the
rotation is legible from outside without opening anything: a ring gear turning
against a pinion is unmistakably a machine at work.

It also solves the family problem. The Ballast Drill, the Drop Crusher and this
building all lean on gravity, and the spec wants them to read as a family -- but
a family of three identical grey boxes is not a family, it is a repetition. A
lying cylinder is the same argument in a different sentence.

## What it risks

**A drum can read as a mixer, a kiln or a cement truck**, all of which are
about combining rather than separating. The two-diameter shell -- fat at the feed
end, narrow past the mesh -- is what says *sorting*, and it has to survive.

**A cylinder at 3x3 is tight.** The drum has to lie inside the footprint with its
cradle, which means it cannot run the full diagonal, and a generator will want to
draw it longer than the box allows.

# Option C — the Yoke

**One line.** A heavy cast cross-yoke bolted down over a small central plug, each of its four arms ending in its own ground anchor.

## The idea

Separate the seal from the thing holding it. In the middle of the footprint is a
small **plug** — a squat capped boss, no wider than a third of a tile, and that is
all there is of the wellhead itself. Over it lies a **cross-yoke**: two thick cast
beams crossing at right angles, their four arms running out to the four face
midpoints, each ending in a **ground anchor** driven into the crust. Where the
beams cross, a heavy jack screw bears down on the plug's crown.

The **riser** leaves the plug low, runs out beneath one arm of the yoke — sheltered
under it — chokes at the arm's end, and terminates at that tile edge.

Seen from above, the machine is an X on a square, which is the one shape in this
set that a player will recognise from the map at a glance.

## Why it suits this machine

**The mechanism is legible without opening anything.** A jack screw pressing a
cap down, held by four anchors, is a complete explanation of "holding something
back" in parts anyone can name. Option A implies the same thing with bolts; this
one shows the load path.

**A cross is the strongest 2 x 2 silhouette available.** A 2 x 2 building has very
little shape to work with, and this one spends all of it on a form that reads
from directly overhead — where the camera is. The arms also run to the face
midpoints, which keeps the whole thing axis-aligned by construction.

**It gives the riser somewhere to be.** Under an arm, the pipe is protected,
short, and unmistakably part of the machine rather than a tube attached to it.

## What it risks

**It is the one option that is deliberately open**, and open is the shape of the
anti-read. Vanilla's offshore pump is a light frame with gaps in it, and this is a
frame with gaps in it. The whole defence is *weight*: the arms are thick cast
beams with ribs and fillets, not struts; there are no diagonal braces; and nothing
about it looks like it could be lifted. If the sheet comes back with slender arms,
the option has failed on exactly the axis it was risking.

**An X on the ground is a marking.** Painted crosses, drain gratings, hatch
markers and landing pads all look like this from above, and a 2 x 2 object at
ground level has little else to say otherwise. The plug's height and the anchors'
bulk are what make it an object rather than a decal.

**The plug is small, and it is where both temperatures live.** The ground seam is
at the plug's foot and the choke is at an arm's end, which is further apart than
on any other option here — and §3.3 says the adjacency *is* the signature. The
prompt pulls the choke back toward the middle of the arm to close the gap, and if
that is not enough this option loses its signature feature.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Crust Tap -- option C, the Yoke.
A very low two-by-two wellhead: a small capped plug over the bore, held down by a
heavy cast cross-yoke anchored at all four arms.

== FORM ==
- In the CENTRE of the 2x2 footprint, a small squat PLUG: a capped cast boss no
  wider than a third of a tile, standing about a quarter of a tile tall. This is
  the whole wellhead, and it is CAPPED -- nothing can be seen down it.
- Over it, a CROSS-YOKE: two THICK CAST BEAMS crossing at right angles, their
  four arms running out to the four FACE MIDPOINTS of the footprint. The arms are
  heavy beams with cast ribs and fillets, deep in section -- never slender struts
  and never diagonal braces.
- At the end of each arm, a GROUND ANCHOR: a bolt plate driven into the crust with
  a washer stack and a tie rod. Four of them, one per face.
- Where the beams cross, a heavy JACK SCREW bearing down on the plug's crown, with
  wrench flats and a lock nut -- the load path from yoke to plug, visible
- A small bulging BURST DISC on the plug's shoulder -- a thin domed plate in a
  ring of small bolts, plainly a safety afterthought
- One RISER leaving the plug low and running out BENEATH one arm of the yoke,
  sheltered under it: it narrows sharply at a CHOKE about halfway along the arm --
  visibly thinner at the throat than at either end -- and terminates at the middle
  of that tile edge, at ground level
- RIME standing on the riser DOWNSTREAM of the choke only
- A thin DULL ORANGE LINE of conducted heat in the joint where the plug meets the
  ground

== COLOUR ==
- Yoke, plug and anchors: #3E3B36 to #5E584E, dark warm iron-nickel, cast and
  bolted
- Riser body: #8A8580, pale grey, the single pipe
- Rime: #BFD8E8 to #EAF4FA, on the riser downstream of the choke ONLY
- Seam heat: #C8541E to #E8A24A, in the plug's ground joint ONLY, dull and narrow
- Burst disc: #C8A23A, one small plate
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on the jack
  screw's collar, tie-rod ends, anchor fittings, gland nuts and earthing straps.
  This is what keeps the building warm. Do not ration it.
- WARM dark metal, one COLD pipe and one DULL HOT seam. The yoke must not drift
  blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0, the Foothold -- the first thing built on this planet, made on site from what was to hand. Crude and mechanical: cast housings, riveted and bolted plate, ground anchors, exposed screw threads, weld seams, honest wear, a burst disc that is plainly an afterthought. Nothing sealed, nothing seamless, no chamfered composite shells, no cryogenic jacketing, no indicator lamps, no light used as a material.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere, and NOTHING ESCAPING: no gas plume, no jet, no
  vapour, no wisp, no haze, no spray. This building's whole argument is that it is
  HOLDING SOMETHING BACK, and a visible leak is the design contradicting itself.
- THE BORE IS CAPPED. No shaft, no hole, no opening, no visible interior, no far
  inner wall, nothing to look down -- and nothing can be seen through the gaps
  between the yoke's arms except the ground the machine stands on.
- THE ARMS RUN TO THE FACE MIDPOINTS, straight along the two axes, never on the
  diagonals. The footprint reads as a SQUARE.
- ONE FLUID CONNECTION: the riser's tip. It stays at GROUND LEVEL and stops flush
  at the middle of a tile edge, square to that face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal.
  It does not climb over the yoke, it does not stop in mid-air and it does not
  cross the edge of the footprint. It is drawn into the building's own art.
- NO item ports of any kind, no chutes, no bins, no hatches aimed at a tile: no
  inserter ever touches this machine. NO electrical connector and no cables
  either -- this machine draws no power at all.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 2x2 footprint, arms, anchors and riser
  included. Two tiles is small and the arms are what will cross the line; the
  anchors sit ON the footprint edge, not beyond it.
- NO GROUND IS DRAWN: no scorch ring, no ground plane, no gravel, no rock, no
  scenery, no baked drop shadow. The machine is drawn on its own.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, anywhere.
- THE HEAT IS UNDERGROUND AND STAYS THERE. The only warm thing on the machine is
  the thin dull orange line of CONDUCTED HEAT in the plug's ground joint. It is
  dull, narrow, at ground level only, and it is heat through metal -- never a
  flame, never molten anything, never bright.
- THE RIME IS COLD AND CLOSE. Pale frost forms on the BARE PIPE downstream of the
  choke, because gas that expands gets cold. It is rime on metal, not a
  manufactured jacket or a fitted sleeve. The choke sits halfway along the arm so
  the rime is close to the hot ground seam: that ADJACENCY is the signature and
  both must be legible in one glance.
- IT IS HEAVY, NOT FRAMED. The arms are deep cast beams; there are no lattice
  members, no tubular struts, no trusses and no diagonal bracing anywhere.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as vanilla's offshore pump -- no water
  anywhere, no impeller, no rotor, no light open frame, nothing that looks like
  it could be lifted by hand. It must NOT read as a lava tap, a magma vent or a
  volcano cap: nothing molten, nothing pouring, nothing bright, and no orange
  above ground level. And it must NOT read as a painted marking, a drain grating,
  a landing pad or a survey marker: it stands proud of the ground, it casts a
  real form, and the jack screw at its centre is plainly a working part.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- A FOUR-ROTATION ROW labelled N, E, S, W: the SAME camera in all four frames with the building turned underneath it, the riser running out beneath a different arm in each. Never a front or a side elevation. Everything except the riser is identical in all four.
- One close-up detail panel: the crossing of the two beams with the jack screw bearing on the plug's crown, the plug's dull orange ground seam beneath it, and the choked riser with its rime leaving to one side
- A two-frame state pair: dormant and venting -- the ground seam dark and the riser bare, then the seam a dull orange line and rime standing on the riser beyond the choke. Nothing escapes in either frame.
- A palette strip of six colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet CRUST TAP -- YOKE -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

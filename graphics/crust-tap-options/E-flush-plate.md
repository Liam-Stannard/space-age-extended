# Option E — the Flush Plate

**One line.** Almost nothing above ground: an armoured plate at grade with a raised rim, and a low horizontal riser running just above it to the tile edge.

## The idea

Find out how little is enough. The building is a single armoured **plate** laid
flat over the bore, filling the 2 x 2 and standing barely a tenth of a tile proud
of the ground. Around its edge is a **raised rim** — a thick cast lip with a ring
of countersunk bolts through it into the crust — and set flush into the plate,
off-centre, is a **burst disc** and a small capped inspection boss.

The **riser** is the only thing with any height at all, and it has very little: it
leaves the plate low, chokes, and runs horizontally a few pixels above the surface
to terminate at the tile edge. Rime stands on it past the choke, and the dull
orange seam runs right round the plate's rim, immediately beneath.

## Why it suits this machine

**§4 says the interesting thing about this building is where it is, not how tall
it is.** This is the only option that takes that sentence literally. Every other
design here adds mass to say *containment*; this one says it by being pressed
flat, the way a real high-pressure cap is.

**The adjacency is the whole building.** The hot rim seam and the cold riser are
within a few pixels of each other because there is nowhere else for either of them
to be. §3.3 calls that adjacency the signature; here it is not one feature among
several, it is the only feature.

**It is the cheapest thing to sit a vent tile under.** §20 already lists the crust
vent tile's art as a second job inside this brief, and a nearly-flat building is
the one that lets that tile do the visual work — the scorch, the fracture pattern,
whatever the tile turns out to be. Every taller option covers its own site.

## What it risks

**It may not read as a building at all.** A flat armoured disc at grade is a
manhole, a drain, an inspection cover or a decal, and at map zoom a 2 x 2 object
with a tenth of a tile of height has essentially no silhouette. This is the
deliberate experiment of the round, and the silhouette panel decides it: if the
flat black shape is a plain square with a stub on it, the option is dead and that
is worth knowing for the price of one generation rather than three stages.

**It is also the option most likely to be lost under things.** The player will run
pipes to it, and a building that is barely proud of the ground will be visually
swallowed by its own plumbing.

**Nowhere to hide a lack of detail.** A flat plate is the same problem the Whisker
Comber's smooth drum had — 0.114 edge density against vanilla's 0.144 floor — with
less area to fix it in. The rim bolts, the plate's panel breaks and the riser's
fittings are all it has.

**And the burst disc has to survive being flush.** §3.2 wants it *"bulging"*,
which is a profile feature, and a profile feature on a flat plate seen from
almost directly above is nearly invisible. Drawn as a raised dome in a recessed
well is the compromise the prompt asks for.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Crust Tap -- option E, the Flush Plate.
A two-by-two wellhead pressed almost flat: an armoured plate at ground level over
the bore, with one low horizontal riser leaving a side.

== FORM ==
- A single ARMOURED PLATE laid flat over the bore, filling the whole 2x2
  footprint and standing barely a tenth of a tile proud of the ground. This is
  the entire body of the building.
- Around its edge, a RAISED RIM: a thick cast lip with a ring of countersunk
  bolts driven through it into the crust
- The plate's face is broken into machined panels with recessed fastener wells,
  a small capped INSPECTION BOSS off-centre, and cast lifting slots
- A BURST DISC set into a recessed well in the plate: a small raised dome inside
  a machined pocket, so it still reads as a bulge from almost directly above --
  plainly a safety afterthought
- One RISER leaving the plate LOW on one side: it narrows sharply at a CHOKE --
  visibly thinner at the throat than at either end -- then runs HORIZONTALLY a
  few pixels above the surface and terminates at the middle of that tile edge, at
  ground level. It is the only part of the machine with any height, and it has
  very little.
- RIME standing on the riser DOWNSTREAM of the choke only
- A thin DULL ORANGE LINE of conducted heat running right round the joint where
  the rim meets the ground, immediately beneath the rime

== COLOUR ==
- Plate and rim: #3E3B36 to #5E584E, dark warm iron-nickel, cast and bolted
- Riser body: #8A8580, pale grey, the single pipe
- Rime: #BFD8E8 to #EAF4FA, on the riser downstream of the choke ONLY
- Seam heat: #C8541E to #E8A24A, in the rim's ground joint ONLY, dull and narrow
- Burst disc: #C8A23A, one small plate
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on the rim's bolt
  heads, the inspection boss's collar, the riser's gland nuts and fittings, and
  earthing straps. This is what keeps the building warm. Do not ration it, and it
  matters most on this design: a flat plate has nothing else carrying colour.
- WARM dark metal, one COLD pipe and one DULL HOT seam. The plate must not drift
  blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0, the Foothold -- the first thing built on this planet, made on site from what was to hand. Crude and mechanical: cast plate, countersunk bolts driven into the ground, weld seams, honest wear, a burst disc that is plainly an afterthought. Nothing sealed, nothing seamless, no chamfered composite shells, no cryogenic jacketing, no indicator lamps, no light used as a material.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere, and NOTHING ESCAPING: no gas plume, no jet, no
  vapour, no wisp, no haze, no spray. This building's whole argument is that it is
  HOLDING SOMETHING BACK, and a visible leak is the design contradicting itself.
- THE BORE IS CAPPED. No shaft, no hole, no opening, no visible interior, no far
  inner wall, nothing to look down.
- ONE FLUID CONNECTION: the riser's tip. It stays at GROUND LEVEL and stops flush
  at the middle of a tile edge, square to that face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal.
  It does not rise over the plate, it does not stop in mid-air and it does not
  cross the edge of the footprint. It is drawn into the building's own art.
- NO item ports of any kind, no chutes, no bins, no hatches aimed at a tile: no
  inserter ever touches this machine. NO electrical connector and no cables
  either -- this machine draws no power at all.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 2x2 footprint, riser included. Two tiles is
  small and the riser is what will cross the line; keep it inside.
- NO GROUND IS DRAWN: no scorch ring, no ground plane, no gravel, no rock, no
  scenery, no baked drop shadow. The machine is drawn on its own.
- IT IS AN OBJECT, NOT A SURFACE. The plate stands proud of the ground with its
  own rim, its own thickness and its own shading, and the riser gives it a
  profile. It must be recognisable as a machine standing ON the ground, never as
  a texture, a tile, a decal or a marking painted on it.
- THE PLATE IS DENSE. A flat surface gives the eye nothing to hold, so cover it
  in hardware: panel breaks, recessed fastener wells, bolt rings, lifting slots,
  the inspection boss.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, anywhere.
- THE HEAT IS UNDERGROUND AND STAYS THERE. The only warm thing on the machine is
  the thin dull orange line of CONDUCTED HEAT in the rim's ground joint. It is
  dull, narrow, at ground level only, and it is heat through metal -- never a
  flame, never molten anything, never bright.
- THE RIME IS COLD AND CLOSE. Pale frost forms on the BARE PIPE downstream of the
  choke, because gas that expands gets cold. It is rime on metal, not a
  manufactured jacket or a fitted sleeve. It sits within a few pixels of the hot
  rim seam: that ADJACENCY is the signature and both must be legible in one
  glance.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as vanilla's offshore pump -- no water
  anywhere, no impeller, no rotor, no light open frame, nothing that looks like
  it could be lifted by hand. It must NOT read as a lava tap, a magma vent or a
  volcano cap: nothing molten, nothing pouring, nothing bright, and no orange
  above ground level. And it must NOT read as a manhole cover, a drain, an
  inspection hatch, a floor tile or a helipad marking: the rim's driven bolts,
  the burst disc, the choked riser and the ground seam are what make it a
  machine.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- A FOUR-ROTATION ROW labelled N, E, S, W: the SAME camera in all four frames with the building turned underneath it, the riser leaving a different face in each. Never a front or a side elevation. Everything except the riser is identical in all four.
- One close-up detail panel: the rim where the riser leaves -- the countersunk bolts, the choke, the rime beyond it, and the dull orange ground seam immediately beneath
- A two-frame state pair: dormant and venting -- the ground seam dark and the riser bare, then the seam a dull orange line and rime standing on the riser beyond the choke. Nothing escapes in either frame.
- A palette strip of six colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet CRUST TAP -- FLUSH PLATE -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

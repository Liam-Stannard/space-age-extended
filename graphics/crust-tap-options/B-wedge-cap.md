# Option B — the Wedge Cap

**One line.** A solid cast frustum driven over the bore: no anchors, no bolts holding it down — the mass is the seal.

## The idea

Take the anchors away and let weight do the work. The building is one **solid cast
block** shaped as a truncated pyramid: a square base filling the 2 x 2, sloping
inward on all four sides to a small flat crown about a third of the width. It has
been driven down over the bore like a plug into a bottle, and the sides are
scarred where it was driven.

The crown carries a bolt ring and a small **burst disc**. The **riser** leaves low
on one sloped face, chokes immediately, and runs down the slope to the tile edge —
so the pipe is short, and the choke and the ground seam are almost touching.

## Why it suits this machine

**It is the strongest possible answer to the offshore pump anti-read.** Vanilla's
is a light open frame; this is the heaviest object in the mod. There is no frame,
no mechanism, no moving part and nothing that could be lifted — the whole machine
is one lump of cast metal.

**It says "driven in", which is a different verb from "bolted on".** A tier 0
building made on landing day, out of what was to hand, plausibly gets seated by
being hammered down rather than by being fastened, and the drive scars on the
sloped faces are honest tier 0 wear.

**And the adjacency is the tightest of the five.** The riser leaves at the bottom
of a slope, so its choke sits within a couple of pixels of the ground joint. §3.3
says that adjacency *is* the signature; this is the design that makes it hardest
to get wrong.

## What it risks

**A taper is directional, and directional forms get turned.** The template records
the Dross Classifier coming back corner-on because a generator wanted to show a
slope off, and this option is nothing but slope. The defence is that the taper is
symmetric about both axes, so there is no "high end" to turn toward the camera —
but the base still has to read as a square, and the prompt says so twice.

**Four sloped faces meeting a small crown is a pyramid, and a pyramid is not a
machine.** It could read as a monument, a bollard, a plinth or a paperweight. The
drive scars, the crown bolt ring, the burst disc and the riser are the only things
saying otherwise, and there are not many of them — this is the least detailed
option in the set by construction, which is a real risk against vanilla's 0.144
edge-density floor.

**No anchors means losing §3.2's first feature.** The spec's opening bullet is
*"an armoured collar, low and heavy, clamped down with visible ground anchors —
this thing is holding something in."* This option keeps the sentiment and drops
the mechanism, and it should be judged on whether mass alone carries it.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Crust Tap -- option B, the Wedge Cap.
A very low two-by-two wellhead drawn as one solid cast wedge driven over a bore,
sealed by its own weight rather than by fasteners.

== FORM ==
- ONE SOLID CAST BLOCK shaped as a truncated pyramid: a SQUARE base filling the
  whole 2x2 footprint, sloping inward on all four sides to a small flat CROWN
  about a third of the width. It stands about half a tile tall.
- The taper is SYMMETRIC about both axes: there is no high end and no low end,
  and the base is square.
- DRIVE SCARS on the sloped faces near the top: peened, burred and battered metal
  where the block was hammered down, plus cast ribs running down the slopes
- The CROWN carries a bolt ring around a capped centre. Nothing can be seen down
  it.
- A small bulging BURST DISC set on the crown beside the bolt ring -- a thin domed
  plate in a ring of small bolts, plainly a safety afterthought
- One RISER leaving LOW on one sloped face: it narrows sharply at a CHOKE
  immediately -- visibly thinner at the throat than at either end -- then runs
  down the slope and terminates at the middle of that tile edge, at ground level
- RIME standing on the riser DOWNSTREAM of the choke only
- A thin DULL ORANGE LINE of conducted heat in the joint where the block's base
  meets the ground, a few pixels below the rime

== COLOUR ==
- Cast block: #3E3B36 to #5E584E, dark warm iron-nickel, cast and battered
- Riser body: #8A8580, pale grey, the single pipe
- Rime: #BFD8E8 to #EAF4FA, on the riser downstream of the choke ONLY
- Seam heat: #C8541E to #E8A24A, in the ground joint ONLY, dull and narrow
- Burst disc: #C8A23A, one small plate
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on fittings, the
  crown's bolt ring, gland nuts and the riser's collars. This is what keeps the
  building warm. Do not ration it, and it matters on this design: a plain cast
  wedge has nothing else carrying colour.
- WARM dark metal, one COLD pipe and one DULL HOT seam. The block must not drift
  blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0, the Foothold -- the first thing built on this planet, made on site from what was to hand. Crude and mechanical: cast housings, riveted and bolted plate, weld seams, honest wear, a burst disc that is plainly an afterthought. Nothing sealed, nothing seamless, no chamfered composite shells, no cryogenic jacketing, no indicator lamps, no light used as a material.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere, and NOTHING ESCAPING: no gas plume, no jet, no
  vapour, no wisp, no haze, no spray. This building's whole argument is that it is
  HOLDING SOMETHING BACK, and a visible leak is the design contradicting itself.
- THE BORE IS CAPPED. No shaft, no hole, no opening, no visible interior, no far
  inner wall, nothing to look down.
- ONE FLUID CONNECTION: the riser's tip. It comes down to GROUND LEVEL and stops
  flush at the middle of a tile edge, square to that face. Axis-aligned, never
  diagonal. It does not leave the crown, it does not stop in mid-air and it does
  not cross the edge of the footprint. It is drawn into the building's own art.
- NO item ports of any kind, no chutes, no bins, no hatches aimed at a tile: no
  inserter ever touches this machine. NO electrical connector and no cables
  either -- this machine draws no power at all.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 2x2 footprint, riser included. Two tiles is
  small and the riser is what will cross the line; keep it inside.
- THE BASE READS AS A SQUARE. The taper is in the sides only. Do not rotate the
  machine to show the slope off; there is no correct corner-on view of this
  building.
- NO GROUND IS DRAWN: no scorch ring, no ground plane, no gravel, no rock, no
  scenery, no baked drop shadow. The machine is drawn on its own.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, anywhere.
- THE HEAT IS UNDERGROUND AND STAYS THERE. The only warm thing on the machine is
  the thin dull orange line of CONDUCTED HEAT in the ground joint. It is dull,
  narrow, at ground level only, and it is heat through metal -- never a flame,
  never molten anything, never bright.
- THE RIME IS COLD AND CLOSE. Pale frost forms on the BARE PIPE downstream of the
  choke, because gas that expands gets cold. It is rime on metal, not a
  manufactured jacket or a fitted sleeve. It sits within a few pixels of the hot
  ground seam: that ADJACENCY is the signature and both must be legible in one
  glance.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as vanilla's offshore pump -- no water
  anywhere, no impeller, no rotor, no light open frame, nothing that looks like
  it could be lifted by hand. It must NOT read as a lava tap, a magma vent or a
  volcano cap: nothing molten, nothing pouring, nothing bright, and no orange
  above ground level. And it must NOT read as a monument, a plinth, a bollard, a
  pyramid or a paperweight: the drive scars, the crown bolt ring, the burst disc
  and the choked riser are what make it a machine.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- A FOUR-ROTATION ROW labelled N, E, S, W: the SAME camera in all four frames with the building turned underneath it, the riser leaving a different face in each. Never a front or a side elevation. Everything except the riser is identical in all four.
- One close-up detail panel: the foot of the slope where the riser leaves -- the choke, the rime beyond it, and the dull orange ground seam a few pixels below
- A two-frame state pair: dormant and venting -- the ground seam dark and the riser bare, then the seam a dull orange line and rime standing on the riser beyond the choke. Nothing escapes in either frame.
- A palette strip of six colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet CRUST TAP -- WEDGE CAP -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

# Option C — the Twin Bottles

**One line.** Two unequal sealed vessels standing side by side, one dark and hot, one pale and frosted, bridged by a short heavy cross-over at the waist.

## The idea

Split the machine in half and let the two halves be different temperatures. A
tall dark **hot bottle** stands on the right of the footprint — heavy, ribbed,
its base skirt joints dull orange — and a shorter, fatter, frost-jacketed **cold
bottle** stands on the left. Between them, at about waist height, a single short,
thick, lagged **cross-over** runs from one to the other: melt goes in one side,
gas comes off the other, and the cross-over is where the transfer happens.

The two vessels are visibly unequal — different heights, different diameters,
different finishes. That inequality is the whole design.

## Why it suits this machine

**It puts the split at the boundary between two objects, which is the strongest
form of it.** Options A and B put a line on one body; this makes hot and cold
two separate things you can point at, and the cross-over is a physical
explanation of what the machine does rather than a decoration on it.

**Two unequal cylinders is a silhouette nothing else in the set has.** The Coil
Separator is one vessel and a fin stack. The Whisker Comber is one standing drum.
Two bottles of different heights, seen as a flat black shape, are immediately not
either of those.

**It solves the port geometry cleanly.** Melt enters the hot bottle on the near
face, gas leaves the cold bottle on the far face, melt returns from the hot
bottle on the left — three faces, three ports, each one attached to the vessel it
belongs to and none of them running across the building to get there. That is
worth something: §8 calls three ports on three faces the geometry to get right at
concept stage.

## What it risks

**Two vessels side by side is a chemical plant**, and the chemical plant is the
attached style reference, which makes the pull toward it stronger rather than
weaker. Vanilla's has two tanks and a lot of pipework, and this design has two
tanks and one pipe. The defence is that this one has almost no external
pipework — one cross-over and three flanges — and that the two vessels are
different *temperatures*, which vanilla's are not.

**It may read as a fuel depot or a tank farm**, which is the same failure in a
different costume: a pair of tanks with a walkway between them is infrastructure,
not a machine. The cross-over must be plainly a working part, thick and lagged
and clamped, and there must be no ladders, no walkway and no handrail anywhere on
the sheet.

**The footprint is tight.** Two vessels plus a cross-over inside 3 x 3 with no
sideways overhang is the hardest packing problem of the five, and a generator
handed two round objects will let one of them spill over the edge. The prompt
says the width limit twice for that reason.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Helium Concentrator -- option C, the Twin Bottles.
A three-by-three sealed gas separator built as two unequal vessels of different
temperatures, bridged at the waist.

== FORM ==
- TWO sealed vessels standing side by side on one chamfered base plate that
  fills the 3x3 footprint. They are visibly UNEQUAL: different heights,
  different diameters, different finishes.
- RIGHT-HAND vessel, the HOT one: taller and narrower, dark, ribbed
  horizontally, standing on a heavy skirt whose joints show dull orange
  incandescence from inside. Sealed domed head, no hatch.
- LEFT-HAND vessel, the COLD one: shorter and fatter, pale frost-jacketed over a
  dark shell, banded with jacket seams and capped with a chamfered head
- Between them at about WAIST HEIGHT, one short THICK LAGGED CROSS-OVER: a
  single heavy insulated duct with clamped flanges at both ends, the only thing
  joining the two vessels. Nothing else spans the gap -- no walkway, no
  handrail, no ladder.
- Frost begins on the cold vessel and on the cross-over's left half only, and
  stops at the clamp in the middle of the cross-over
- Near face, centre: a WIDE DARK inlet flange at ground level, at the foot of
  the HOT vessel
- Far face, centre: a slim pale frost-jacketed GAS OUTLET leaving the COLD
  vessel's head, turning over, running down its flank and terminating at a
  flange at ground level
- Left face, centre: a HEAVY INSULATED outlet line leaving the HOT vessel's
  skirt low, at ground level

== COLOUR ==
- Body and shell: #4A463F to #6E685C, warm low-value iron-nickel, sealed and
  chamfered
- Melt heat and melt lines: #7A4A28 to #C86A2E, on the HOT vessel's skirt joints
  only, dull and contained
- Frost and gas pipe: #BFD8E8 to #EAF4FA, on the COLD vessel, the cross-over's
  left half and the gas pipe
- Shielding collars: #3B3B40, near-black, clamped at all three ports and at both
  ends of the cross-over
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs,
  joints, flange faces and fittings. This is what keeps the building warm.
  Do not ration it.
- WARM metal, one COLD vessel and one HOT vessel. The shells must not drift blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 3, the Core's own goods -- advanced, quiet, sealed. Seamless welded shells, chamfered forms, no visible fasteners on the primary faces, cryogenic jacketing where it is earned, machinery implied rather than shown. No rivets, no rust, no exposed gears or linkages. But it is HEAVY and SHORT: it stands on the heaviest world in the game and holds molten metal still, so it is squat and dense, never tall.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, powder, grit, melt, gas, debris or
  product on the ground, at ports, at flanges, in trays, or spilling from the
  machine. Factorio machines never show what they make, so every port, flange
  and opening is drawn as EMPTY machinery.
- Both vessels are enclosed. No open melt surface anywhere, no window into
  either one.
- EXACTLY THREE FLUID CONNECTIONS TO THE OUTSIDE WORLD and no more, one on each
  of three different faces, as listed above. The cross-over between the two
  vessels is internal and does not count and must not reach an edge. Each
  outside connection runs down to GROUND LEVEL and stops flush at the middle of
  its tile edge, square to that face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal. No pipe
  leaves the roof, no pipe stops in mid-air, no pipe crosses the edge of the
  footprint. A riser may climb a vessel only if it comes back down and
  terminates at the edge. Each flange is drawn into the building's own art.
- NO item ports of any kind: this machine moves only fluids. No chutes, no bins,
  no hatches aimed at a tile. No visible electrical connector either -- power
  arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 3x3 footprint, pipework included. BOTH
  vessels and the cross-over sit entirely inside it. Height above it is fine;
  width is not.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, no vapour
  plume, anywhere.
- HOT IS NOT FIRE. The heat at the hot vessel's skirt is INCANDESCENT METAL seen
  through narrow joints, and conducted heat glowing dull in seams. It comes from
  inside and it is contained. No flame, no combustion, no furnace mouth.
- THE TEMPERATURE SPLIT IS BETWEEN THE TWO VESSELS: dull emissive orange at the
  hot one's foot, pale frost on the cold one, and nothing warm on the cold
  vessel at all. Copper and brass are a MATERIAL and never glow; the only
  emissive orange on this building is the melt heat at the hot vessel's skirt.
- NO GREEN anywhere. Green belongs to another material in this mod and this gas
  is not that.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as a distillation column or a refinery tower
  -- no fractionating trays, no ladder cage, no flare stack. It must NOT read as
  a tank farm, a fuel depot or a pair of storage tanks: there are no ladders, no
  walkways, no handrails and no external pipe runs beyond the three named
  flanges and the one cross-over. And it must not read as vanilla's chemical
  plant, which is attached only as a camera and finish reference.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: the cross-over's mid-span clamp, with frost on the left half and bare warm metal on the right; and the hot vessel's skirt joint with dull orange contained inside it
- A two-frame state pair: the machine cold and running -- both vessels dark and dry, then the hot vessel's skirt joints dull orange and the cold vessel heavily frosted, with nothing warm on the cold vessel in either frame
- A palette strip of six colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet HELIUM CONCENTRATOR -- TWIN BOTTLES -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

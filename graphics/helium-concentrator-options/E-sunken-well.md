# Option E — the Sunken Well

**One line.** Almost no height at all: a machined roof deck filling the footprint, with a frost-ringed capped well set into it and the heat showing only at the skirt where the deck meets the ground.

## The idea

Put the machine underground and draw the lid. The building is a single heavy
**deck plate** covering the whole 3 x 3, standing about a quarter of a tile above
the ground on a chamfered skirt. Set into that deck, slightly off-centre, is a
circular **capped well** — a recessed ring with a flush chamfered plug in it,
frost heavy on the ring's rim and on the plug's edge. The rest of the deck is
instrument bosses, sealed inspection lenses and a copper bus run crossing to the
gas riser.

The heat is at the **skirt**: a dull orange line in the joint where the deck's
skirt meets the ground, running right round the building. Hot below, cold on top,
and the two are a quarter of a tile apart.

## Why it suits this machine

**It is the only option that puts the signature where the camera actually
looks.** A 3 x 3 Factorio building is mostly roof; A's belt and C's cross-over
are on the flanks and are foreshortened away. This design has a frost ring on the
roof and a heat line at ground level, and both are fully visible from the game's
own angle.

**It is the only option that cannot be a distillation column**, because it has no
height to make one out of. §3.1's anti-read costs nothing here.

**It is also the strongest separation from the Coil Separator**, the other tier 3
building on the floor. That one is a standing vessel with a stack; this is a flat
plate. Nothing about the two can be confused, at any zoom.

**And it is the most honest answer to gravity 50.** A machine holding a column of
molten metal on the heaviest world in the game has a reason to be short, and this
is the option that takes that argument all the way.

## What it risks

**It may not read as a building at all.** A flat plate at ground level is a floor
tile, a hatch, a manhole or a decal — and at map zoom a 3 x 3 object with almost
no height has very little silhouette to be recognised by. The silhouette panel is
the whole test of this option, and if it comes back as a plain square, the option
is dead and that is a useful thing to have found for a hundredth of the cost of
finding it at stage 3.

**Nowhere to put the gas riser.** §3.2 wants a riser climbing a flank, and there
is no flank to climb. This design gives it a short stub arch over the far edge of
the deck, which is the one part of the machine with any height at all — and that
stub is then the tallest thing on the building, which risks becoming its whole
read.

**A low building hides its own detail density.** The Whisker Comber's round shell
measured 0.114 against vanilla's 0.144 floor because a smooth surface gives the
eye nothing to hold. A flat deck is the same problem with more area, and the fix
is the same: cover it in hardware.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Helium Concentrator -- option E, the Sunken Well.
A three-by-three sealed gas separator that is almost entirely underground: a
heavy machined deck plate with a frost-ringed capped well set into it.

== FORM ==
- A single heavy machined DECK PLATE covering the whole 3x3 footprint, standing
  only about a quarter of a tile above the ground on a chamfered skirt. This is
  the entire body of the building; there is nothing tall on it.
- Set into the deck, slightly OFF-CENTRE: a circular capped WELL -- a recessed
  ring about a third of the footprint across, with a flush chamfered PLUG shut
  in it. The plug is closed; nothing can be seen through or past it.
- Heavy FROST on the well's ring rim and on the plug's chamfer, and frost
  nowhere else on the deck
- The deck is densely covered in hardware: sealed instrument bosses, recessed
  inspection lenses, chamfered panel breaks, clamped collars and a copper bus
  run crossing the deck to the gas outlet
- At GROUND LEVEL, in the joint where the deck's skirt meets the ground, a thin
  DULL ORANGE LINE of conducted heat running right round the building. That is
  the only warm thing on the machine, and it is a quarter of a tile below the
  frost.
- Near face, centre: a WIDE DARK inlet flange at ground level
- Far face, centre: a short pale frost-jacketed GAS STUB arching just above the
  deck's far edge and coming straight back DOWN to a flange at ground level. It
  is low -- no taller than the well's ring -- and it is the only part of the
  machine with any height.
- Left face, centre: a HEAVY INSULATED outlet line leaving the skirt, at ground
  level

== COLOUR ==
- Deck and skirt: #4A463F to #6E685C, warm low-value iron-nickel, sealed and
  chamfered
- Seam heat: #7A4A28 to #C86A2E, in the ground joint at the skirt ONLY, dull and
  contained
- Frost and gas stub: #BFD8E8 to #EAF4FA, on the well ring, the plug chamfer and
  the gas pipe
- Shielding collars: #3B3B40, near-black, clamped at all three ports
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on the deck's bus
  runs, joints, flange faces and fittings. This is what keeps the building warm.
  Do not ration it.
- WARM metal, a COLD deck and a HOT ground joint. The deck must not drift blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 3, the Core's own goods -- advanced, quiet, sealed. Seamless welded shells, chamfered forms, no visible fasteners on the primary faces, cryogenic jacketing where it is earned, machinery implied rather than shown. No rivets, no rust, no exposed gears or linkages. But it is HEAVY: it stands on the heaviest world in the game and holds molten metal still, and being short is the point of this design rather than an accident of it.
- THE DECK IS DENSE. A flat surface gives the eye nothing to hold, so cover it in
  hardware: instrument bosses, panel breaks, recessed lenses, clamped collars,
  bus runs. Detail density matters more on this design than on any other.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, powder, grit, melt, gas, debris or
  product on the ground, at ports, at flanges, in trays, or spilling from the
  machine. Factorio machines never show what they make, so every port, flange
  and opening is drawn as EMPTY machinery.
- The working chamber is enclosed and it is UNDERGROUND. The well's plug is SHUT
  -- no shaft, no visible interior, no far inner wall, nothing to look into.
- EXACTLY THREE FLUID CONNECTIONS and no more, one on each of three different
  faces, as listed above. Each runs down to GROUND LEVEL and stops flush at the
  middle of its tile edge, square to that face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal.
  No pipe leaves the roof, no pipe stops in mid-air, no pipe crosses the edge of
  the footprint. Each flange is drawn into the building's own art.
- NO item ports of any kind: this machine moves only fluids. No chutes, no bins,
  no hatches aimed at a tile. No visible electrical connector either -- power
  arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 3x3 footprint, pipework included. Nothing is
  drawn on the ground outside the footprint either: no scorch ring, no ground
  plane, no scenery, no cabling.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, no vapour
  plume, anywhere.
- HOT IS NOT FIRE. The heat at the skirt is CONDUCTED HEAT glowing dull in a
  ground joint, coming from what is buried underneath. No flame, no combustion,
  no furnace mouth, nothing bright.
- THE TEMPERATURE SPLIT IS VERTICAL AND VERY SHORT: dull emissive orange in the
  ground joint, heavy frost on the deck a quarter of a tile above it, and
  nothing warm on the deck at all. Copper and brass are a MATERIAL and never
  glow; the only emissive orange on this building is the seam heat at the skirt.
- NO GREEN anywhere. Green belongs to another material in this mod and this gas
  is not that.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as a distillation column or a refinery tower
  -- there is nothing tall on this building at all. And it must NOT read as a
  floor tile, a concrete pad, a manhole cover, a hatch in the ground or a
  decal: it is a machine standing ON the ground with its own chamfered skirt,
  its own shadow and a raised well ring, and it must be recognisable as an
  object rather than as a surface.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: the well's ring and shut plug with frost heavy on the rim; and the skirt's ground joint with the thin dull orange heat line in it
- A two-frame state pair: the machine cold and running -- the ground joint dark and the frost thin, then the joint dull orange and the well ring heavily frosted, with nothing warm on the deck in either frame
- A palette strip of six colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet HELIUM CONCENTRATOR -- SUNKEN WELL -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

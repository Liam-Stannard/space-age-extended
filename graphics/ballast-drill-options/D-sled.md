# Option D — the Sled

**One line.** No legs, no rails and no stroke: the chassis IS the ballast, a single armoured skid pressing its head into the crust by sitting on it.

**What moves:** **nothing.** This is the option that takes the template's other honest answer
— *decide the machine is still and say so* — and it is in the round to be argued
with rather than to be safe.

## The idea

Stop moving a weight around and just be the weight.

One armoured **skid**: a single heavy riveted casting filling the 5 × 5, thicker
at the front than the back, with runner rails along its underside and the cutting
head shrouded beneath its middle. There are no legs, no rails and no block —
the machine's mass *is* its chassis, and it works by sitting on the crust at fifty
gravities.

What it has instead of a stroke is **detail at rest**: heavy tie-down lugs at each
corner, an inspection hatch, a cable gland, wear plates along the runners, and a
short output boom. It is the flattest, heaviest-looking thing on the planet.

## Why it suits this machine

**It is the only option that is honest about what fifty gravities means.** The
other four lift something and drop it; this one points out that at 50 g you do not
need to lift anything, and the design brief's own line — *"the machine presses
with its own mass instead of hammering"* — describes this and not them.

**The template explicitly sanctions it.** §6.2: *"the honest answers are to
redesign the moving part larger or to decide the machine is still and say so
here."* A still machine chosen deliberately is a different thing from a machine
that failed to get an animation.

**It is the cheapest to produce by a wide margin** — four plates, four shadows, a
lamp, no frames at all — and the cheapest to keep correct.

## What it risks

**A machine that never moves cannot say whether it is working.** The status
lamp becomes the entire running-versus-stopped read, on a 5 × 5 where the lamp is
a few pixels. That is a real cost and it is the reason to be suspicious of this
option.

**Low and featureless is how a building disappears.** At play zoom a flat slab on
the ground risks reading as floor plate, a concrete pad, or a blueprint ghost
rather than a machine. The tie-downs, the boom and the hatch are all it has.

**And it throws away the brief's stated signature.** §3.2 says the descending
block *"is the animation that says this machine is using gravity, and it is the
reason the building reads at a glance"*. This option disagrees with that
sentence. It is here because the disagreement is worth having out loud, not
because it is obviously right.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components.

== BUILDING ==
Ballast Drill -- option D, the Sled.
A FIVE-BY-FIVE mining machine on an airless metal world at fifty gravities. It
does not hammer and it does not spin: it brings weight to bear. Its argument is
mass, so it is low and wide.

== FORM ==
- ONE armoured SKID filling the 5x5: a single heavy riveted casting, thicker at
  the front than the back, sitting flat on the ground with NO legs and NO gap
  beneath it
- RUNNER RAILS along the underside edges, with bolted WEAR PLATES showing honest
  scoring
- A shrouded HEAD RIM beneath the middle of the skid, a heavy skirt, closed
- Four heavy TIE-DOWN LUGS at the corners, with shackles
- An armoured INSPECTION HATCH with locking dogs on the top face, SHUT
- A cable gland and cleated run along one flank to a compact motor housing
- A short stubby OUTPUT BOOM on the north face, centred, drawn empty
- A small round white LAMP LENS on the front face of the skid
- Riveted seams, bolt lines, deep bevels, honest wear. This machine has NO moving
  parts visible anywhere -- no rails, no block, no screws, no gears

== COLOUR ==
- Skid, runners and shroud: #4A463F to #6E685C, dark warm iron-nickel, with the
  deep bevels catching light so the casting reads as THICK
- Wear plates and tie-down lugs: #8A857C, brighter than the body, scored and
  burnished where they have been dragged
- Hazard banding: #C8A23A, on the chassis edge and the foot pads ONLY, a stripe
  and not a decoration
- Lamp lens: #FFFFFF, painted white
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs,
  bearing caps, screw collars, motor housings and bolt lines. This is what keeps
  the building warm. Do not ration it.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0, the landing-day machines, and CRUDE ON PURPOSE. Riveted
  plate, cast housings, exposed gears, screws and rails, weld seams, hazard
  tape, honest wear. Nothing sealed, nothing chamfered, no composite shells, no
  jacketing, no light used as a material.
- THE ANTI-READ IS A DERRICK. No tower, no headframe, no mast, no rotating
  superstructure, nothing reaching upward. This machine is LOW AND WIDE and its
  tallest element is still squat against its own footprint.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, rock, spoil, powder, grit, dust or
  debris on the ground, under the head, at the boom, in trays, or falling from
  the machine. It is a mining machine and it must still be drawn EMPTY.
- FOUR FLUID FLANGES, in vanilla's own big-mining-drill layout so a player who
  has plumbed one already knows this one: ONE on the LEFT face and ONE on the
  RIGHT face, each ONE TILE NORTH OF CENTRE; and TWO on the BOTTOM face, flanking
  the centre tile. NONE on the top face -- that is where the ore comes out.
- Each flange sits SQUARE to the edge it is on and points STRAIGHT out of it,
  stopping flush at the middle of its own tile edge. Axis-aligned, never
  diagonal, never out of a corner, never elbowed across a shoulder.
- KEEP THEM SMALL AND UNREMARKABLE. Nothing is plumbed to this machine today --
  the fluid it will take has not been added yet -- so a flange drawn as a feature
  makes an unplumbed drill look broken. They are fittings, not features. No
  hoses, no visible pipework, no valve trees, no manifolds, no capped stubs.
- ONE OUTPUT BOOM, on the NORTH face, centred, short and stubby, reaching just
  past the edge of the footprint. It is DRAWN EMPTY. There is no second boom and
  no chute anywhere else.
- THE CUTTING HEAD IS PART OF THE MACHINE, not a mark on the ground. Draw it as a
  shrouded rim or skirt on the underside of the chassis. Draw NO disturbed earth,
  no crater, no spoil ring, no ground texture -- the sprite is transparent
  everywhere the building is not.
- NOTHING IS HOT AND NOTHING GLOWS. No furnace light, no ember, no warm light
  anywhere. The white lamp lens is the ONLY light on the building. Copper and
  brass are a MATERIAL and never glow.
- THE LAMP LENS IS PAINTED WHITE. White in the hero view, the top-down view, the
  detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game. It is drawn
  coloured ONLY inside the three-state row.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 5x5 footprint except the output boom. Height
  above it is fine; width is not. No visible electrical connector -- power
  arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: no flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke,
  no dust cloud, nothing blowing about, anywhere.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: not a derrick, not a headframe, not an oil rig, not a rotating
  radar or turret. Not a crane. And not a tall buttressed cylinder -- that
  silhouette belongs to another of our buildings.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: a runner's bolted wear plate, scored and burnished; and a corner tie-down lug with its shackle
- A NORTH-FACE view showing the output boom, drawn empty, reaching just past the footprint
- A TOP-DOWN CONNECTION panel marking all four fluid flanges on their tile edges, small and unremarkable, with nothing plumbed to them
- A FOUR-FRAME MOTION ROW of the same view: the same view four times, identical -- this machine does not move. Label the row STILL BY DESIGN
- A THREE-STATE ROW of the same view, three frames: RUNNING with the lamp lens
  glowing GREEN; BLOCKED with the lamp glowing AMBER; IDLE with the lamp dark and
  the machine still
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet BALLAST DRILL -- SLED -- use
exactly that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only;
no other text anywhere on the sheet. No logos, wordmarks or watermarks anywhere.
```

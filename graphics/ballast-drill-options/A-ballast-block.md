# Option A — the Ballast Block

**One line.** §3.2 as written: an unadorned slab of metal riding four visible guide rails above the cutting head, on splayed bracing legs.

**What moves:** the slab, dropping and rising on its rails — a moving box of roughly
**3.0 × 0.7 tiles, about 60 % of the building's own width**, travelling most of a
tile vertically. Comfortably the clearest stroke of the five, and second only to
the Ring Press on share.

## The idea

§3.2 drawn exactly as written, carried as the fair comparison rather than as
the favourite.

A broad low chassis fills the 5 × 5. Four **splayed bracing legs** reach out to
the corners, each ending in a broad foot pad that is obviously pressing rather
than standing. Above the cutting head rides a **ballast block**: an unadorned
slab, the tallest thing on the machine and still squat, running on four visible
guide rails with wipers and stops.

The head itself is a shrouded rim under the chassis. The output boom is a short
stubby spout on the north face.

## Why it suits this machine

**It is the plainest possible statement of the idea**, and the idea is good:
weight on rails over a head, legs braced against the reaction. Nothing has to be
explained.

**The stroke is unmistakable.** A slab dropping a tile and rising again is the
one motion nobody misreads, and it is what §3.2 calls the reason the building
reads at a glance.

**Splayed legs are a silhouette nothing else on this planet has.** Every other
Core building stands on a square base; this one braces outward, which reads as a
machine pushing against the ground rather than resting on it.

## What it risks

**A plain slab is the thing the Drop Crusher's round already learned to
distrust.** §3.3 of that building had to be corrected — *"the weight must not be
flat black… it read as a hole in the picture rather than a mass of steel"*. The
same warning applies here in the same words, and this option has more slab than
any other.

**Four legs and four rails is a lot of vertical clutter for a machine whose read
is horizontal.** If the rails come back as posts it is a derrick, which is the
anti-read.

**It is the most generic of the five.** A block on rails is what any concept
artist draws for "press". That is not disqualifying, but the other four are all
trying to be something.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components.

== BUILDING ==
Ballast Drill -- option A, the Ballast Block.
A FIVE-BY-FIVE mining machine on an airless metal world at fifty gravities. It
does not hammer and it does not spin: it brings weight to bear. Its argument is
mass, so it is low and wide.

== FORM ==
- A broad LOW CHASSIS filling the 5x5, riveted, with a shrouded head rim on its
  underside
- FOUR SPLAYED BRACING LEGS reaching out to the corners, each ending in a broad
  FOOT PAD pressed against the ground, obviously bracing rather than standing
- A BALLAST BLOCK riding above the head: a heavy unadorned SLAB, the tallest
  element and still squat, with heavily chamfered edges catching the light and a
  scarred burnished underside
- FOUR VISIBLE GUIDE RAILS the block runs on, with wipers, stops and guide shoes
  clearly wrapping each rail
- A compact geared drive housing on one flank, with exposed gears
- A short stubby OUTPUT BOOM on the north face, centred, drawn empty
- A small round white LAMP LENS on the chassis, front face, clear of the rails
- Riveted seams, bolt lines, lifting lugs, cable cleats, honest wear

== COLOUR ==
- Chassis, legs and rails: #4A463F to #6E685C, dark warm iron-nickel
- Ballast block: #2E2C29 body with chamfers up to #6A6660. DARK, but with a REAL
  VALUE RANGE across its chamfers so it reads as a forged mass of steel and NOT
  as a flat black hole in the picture
- Rails, guide shoes and drive gears: #8A857C, brighter than the chassis
- Hazard banding: #C8A23A, on the chassis edge and the foot pads ONLY, a stripe
  and not a decoration
- Lamp lens: #FFFFFF, painted white
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs,
  bearing caps, screw collars, motor housings and bolt lines. This is what keeps
  the building warm. Do not ration it.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0, the landing-day machines, and CRUDE ON PURPOSE. Riveted
  plate, cast housings, exposed gears, screws and rails, weld seams, hazard
  tape, honest wear. Nothing sealed, nothing chamfered, no composite shells, no
  jacketing, no light used as a material.
- THE ANTI-READ IS A DERRICK. No tower, no headframe, no mast, no rotating
  superstructure, nothing reaching upward. This machine is LOW AND WIDE and its
  tallest element is still squat against its own footprint.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, rock, spoil, powder, grit, dust or
  debris on the ground, under the head, at the boom, in trays, or falling from
  the machine. It is a mining machine and it must still be drawn EMPTY.
- DRAW NO FLUID FITTINGS AT ALL: no flange, no pipe stub, no hose, no valve, no
  manifold, no capped port, anywhere on the machine. This is how vanilla draws
  its own drills -- the big mining drill's plate has no plumbing on it whatsoever
  -- and the reason is that the ENGINE stamps the fitting itself at whichever
  connections are live, and draws nothing at all on an ore that needs no fluid.
  A flange painted into the plate would be a socket that is visible even when the
  machine has no use for one.
- ONE OUTPUT BOOM, on the NORTH face, centred, short and stubby, reaching just
  past the edge of the footprint. It is DRAWN EMPTY. There is no second boom and
  no chute anywhere else.
- THE CUTTING HEAD IS PART OF THE MACHINE, not a mark on the ground. Draw it as a
  shrouded rim or skirt on the underside of the chassis. Draw NO disturbed earth,
  no crater, no spoil ring, no ground texture -- the sprite is transparent
  everywhere the building is not.
- NOTHING IS HOT AND NOTHING GLOWS. No furnace light, no ember, no warm light
  anywhere. The white lamp lens is the ONLY light on the building. Copper and
  brass are a MATERIAL and never glow.
- THE LAMP LENS IS PAINTED WHITE. White in the hero view, the top-down view, the
  detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game. It is drawn
  coloured ONLY inside the three-state row.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 5x5 footprint except the output boom. Height
  above it is fine; width is not. No visible electrical connector -- power
  arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: no flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke,
  no dust cloud, nothing blowing about, anywhere.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: not a derrick, not a headframe, not an oil rig, not a rotating
  radar or turret. Not a crane. And not a tall buttressed cylinder -- that
  silhouette belongs to another of our buildings.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: the ballast block's guide shoes wrapping a rail, with the wiper and stop; and the shrouded head rim under the chassis
- A NORTH-FACE view showing the output boom, drawn empty, reaching just past the footprint
- A TOP-DOWN panel with the four fluid connection points MARKED WITH A SYMBOL, not drawn as hardware: the engine supplies the fitting, and this panel exists only to prove the plate leaves those four tile edges clear
- A FOUR-FRAME MOTION ROW of the same view: block at the top of its travel with the rails clear; descending; seated hard down on the head; rising again with the drive gears turning
- A THREE-STATE ROW of the same view, three frames: RUNNING with the lamp lens
  glowing GREEN; BLOCKED with the lamp glowing AMBER; IDLE with the lamp dark and
  the machine still
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet BALLAST DRILL -- BALLAST BLOCK -- use
exactly that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only;
no other text anywhere on the sheet. No logos, wordmarks or watermarks anywhere.
```

# Option E — the Slug Magazine

**One line.** A carousel on the roof drops ballast slugs onto the head one at a time, so the motion is a big turning drum where the camera is already looking.

**What moves:** the magazine drum, turning continuously on the roof — a moving box of roughly
**3.0 × 3.0 tiles, about 60 % of the width** — plus a slug falling through the
chute each cycle. The only option whose motion is on the surface the camera shows
most of.

## The idea

The camera looks down. So put the moving part where it is looking.

A low riveted chassis fills the 5 × 5. On its roof sits a wide **carousel** — a
cast drum with six deep pockets round its rim, each holding a stubby **ballast
slug**, turning slowly like a revolver cylinder. As each pocket comes over the
drop chute the slug falls through onto the cutting head, and the empty pocket
carries on round to be re-loaded from below.

Nothing about the machine reaches up: the carousel is wide and flat, and the
tallest thing on the building is its own rim.

## Why it suits this machine

**It puts its motion on the roof**, which on a steeply-angled camera is most of
what a player sees. Every other option here does its work at the waist or below,
where a 5 × 5's own bulk hides it.

**A turning drum is the cheapest possible animation to keep honest** — one drawn
part rotated about a fixed centre is the motion this project's own tooling derives
cleanly, and it cannot drift the way a redrawn frame can.

**And it explains the drain.** This drill halves the patch it works, and a machine
that spends consumable slugs is the most legible possible picture of a mining
method that costs the ore something. Nothing else in the set says that.

## What it risks

**A six-pocket turning drum on a square base is a revolver, and it is also a
centrifuge**, which is the Dross Classifier's reference. Coarse pockets, visible
slugs and a slow clacking index are the defence; a smooth turning lid is not.

**The slugs are consumable and the recipe has no such item.** Drawing the machine
spending something it does not consume is a fiction the rest of this mod is
careful not to tell — every port is drawn empty precisely so the art never
promises a mechanic the entity lacks. This is the sharpest objection to the option
and it should be weighed before the sheet is judged, not after.

**And a slug falling through a chute is loose material**, which every prompt in
this repo forbids. Here it is machinery rather than product, which is arguably a
different thing, but it is a line worth being deliberate about crossing.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components.

== BUILDING ==
Ballast Drill -- option E, the Slug Magazine.
A FIVE-BY-FIVE mining machine on an airless metal world at fifty gravities. It
does not hammer and it does not spin: it brings weight to bear. Its argument is
mass, so it is low and wide.

== FORM ==
- A broad LOW CHASSIS filling the 5x5, riveted, with a shrouded head rim on its
  underside
- On the roof: a wide cast CAROUSEL almost three tiles across, with SIX deep
  POCKETS round its rim, turning like a revolver cylinder. It is a WHEEL, not a
  lid, and it is flat -- nothing about it reaches up
- A stubby BALLAST SLUG seated in each pocket: dark forged cylinders with
  chamfered ends and scarred faces
- A heavy INDEX PAWL on a bracket bolted to the chassis, riding the carousel's
  rim, standing proud of the roofline
- A guarded DROP CHUTE where a pocket comes over the head, and one slug shown
  mid-fall inside it
- A compact geared motor housing driving the carousel, with exposed gears
- A short stubby OUTPUT BOOM on the north face, centred, drawn empty
- A small round white LAMP LENS on the index pawl's bracket
- Riveted seams, bolt lines, lifting lugs, cable cleats, honest wear

== COLOUR ==
- Chassis, carousel and chute: #4A463F to #6E685C, dark warm iron-nickel
- Ballast slugs: #2E2C29 with chamfered ends up to #6A6660. DARK, with a real
  value range so they read as forged steel and NOT as flat black holes
- Carousel rim, pockets, index pawl and drive gears: #8A857C, brighter than the
  chassis, with dark wear in the pocket mouths
- Hazard banding: #C8A23A, on the chassis edge and the foot pads ONLY, a stripe
  and not a decoration
- Lamp lens: #FFFFFF, painted white
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs,
  bearing caps, screw collars, motor housings and bolt lines. This is what keeps
  the building warm. Do not ration it.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0, the landing-day machines, and CRUDE ON PURPOSE. Riveted
  plate, cast housings, exposed gears, screws and rails, weld seams, hazard
  tape, honest wear. Nothing sealed, nothing chamfered, no composite shells, no
  jacketing, no light used as a material.
- THE ANTI-READ IS A DERRICK. No tower, no headframe, no mast, no rotating
  superstructure, nothing reaching upward. This machine is LOW AND WIDE and its
  tallest element is still squat against its own footprint.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, rock, spoil, powder, grit, dust or
  debris on the ground, under the head, at the boom, in trays, or falling from
  the machine. It is a mining machine and it must still be drawn EMPTY.
- NO PIPES AND NO FLUID CONNECTION ANYWHERE. This machine has no fluid box, so a
  drawn flange would be a socket a player can never plumb. No pipe, no flange, no
  hose, no hydraulic line, no coolant run.
- ONE OUTPUT BOOM, on the NORTH face, centred, short and stubby, reaching just
  past the edge of the footprint. It is DRAWN EMPTY. There is no second boom and
  no chute anywhere else.
- THE CUTTING HEAD IS PART OF THE MACHINE, not a mark on the ground. Draw it as a
  shrouded rim or skirt on the underside of the chassis. Draw NO disturbed earth,
  no crater, no spoil ring, no ground texture -- the sprite is transparent
  everywhere the building is not.
- NOTHING IS HOT AND NOTHING GLOWS. No furnace light, no ember, no warm light
  anywhere. The white lamp lens is the ONLY light on the building. Copper and
  brass are a MATERIAL and never glow.
- THE LAMP LENS IS PAINTED WHITE. White in the hero view, the top-down view, the
  detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game. It is drawn
  coloured ONLY inside the three-state row.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 5x5 footprint except the output boom. Height
  above it is fine; width is not. No visible electrical connector -- power
  arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: no flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke,
  no dust cloud, nothing blowing about, anywhere.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: not a derrick, not a headframe, not an oil rig, not a rotating
  radar or turret. Not a crane. And not a tall buttressed cylinder -- that
  silhouette belongs to another of our buildings.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: the index pawl riding the carousel's rim with the lamp lens on its bracket; and a pocket at the drop chute with its slug about to fall
- A NORTH-FACE view showing the output boom, drawn empty, reaching just past the footprint
- A FOUR-FRAME MOTION ROW of the same view: carousel indexed with a slug over the chute; the slug falling; the pocket empty and the carousel turning on; the next slug arriving over the chute
- A THREE-STATE ROW of the same view, three frames: RUNNING with the lamp lens
  glowing GREEN; BLOCKED with the lamp glowing AMBER; IDLE with the lamp dark and
  the machine still
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet BALLAST DRILL -- SLUG MAGAZINE -- use
exactly that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only;
no other text anywhere on the sheet. No logos, wordmarks or watermarks anywhere.
```

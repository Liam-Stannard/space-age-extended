# Option L — the Emitter Deck

**One line.** A machined deck with four sealed emitter towers at its corners and a service aperture at its centre. 9x9 tiles.

*Set 3: between option B and G. Advanced, but still Factorio.*

## The idea

Option B's arrangement and option G's sealing, with the lattice gone but
the hardware kept. The towers are cast casings with fins and fasteners rather
than open steelwork or blank cones.

It keeps B's best property — four vertical gauges at the corners, readable from
every approach, with the height sited where the footprint is otherwise wasted.

## Why this register

Set 1 (A–E) was tier 0 — riveted, rusted, improvised — which is the vocabulary of
the landing-day machines, not the last building in the game. Set 2 (F–J) went to
tier 4 and overshot: measured against the four vanilla style references,
`tools/check-sheet-style.py` put them at **saturation 0.10–0.12 against vanilla's
0.23–0.49**, roughly half the chroma floor, and at the bottom of vanilla's detail
range. Cold, monochrome and smooth, where Factorio is warm, busy and mechanical.

That was the spec's fault rather than the generator's: set 2 asked for a blue-grey
shell and "almost no copper".

So set 3 keeps the advanced *fabrication* — machined, sealed, chamfered, lamps,
jacketing, light as a material — and puts back Factorio's **warmth and density**:
copper and brass used freely, panel breaks, vents, grilles, service hardware, pipe
runs in the shadows, honest wear. Sealed, not blank.

## The charge display

the translucent window up each tower filling with violet from the base
upward, all four in step, full to the top at 100%

FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: steeply down from above, MOSTLY ROOF
with only a shallow near face. Square to the tile grid. Not rotated corner-on.
Not a flat front elevation.
The four attached sprites are style references only: match the camera, rendering,
finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the design, shape or

== BUILDING ==
Ignition Array - option L, Emitter Deck.
A machined deck with four sealed emitter towers at its corners and
a service aperture at its centre. 9x9 tiles.

== FORM ==
- A low, thick deck of machined plate with visible panel breaks and a chamfered
  edge, anchored at its corners
- Four sealed emitter towers, one per corner, each about half the deck's width
  in height, thick at the base and blunt on top
- Each tower is a machined casing with cooling fins down its outer face, a bolt
  ring at its base, and a tall narrow window of pale translucent composite up
  its inner face
- Heavy cable runs and coolant lines crossing the deck between the towers
- A circular service aperture at the centre of the deck with a grille over it
- Indicator lamps at each tower base, unlit

== COLOUR ==
- Body casing: #4A463F to #6E685C, WARM low-value iron-nickel, machined not cast
- Pale nickel-white: #B9B4A8, on chamfers, covers and end caps
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs, joints and
  fittings. This is what keeps the building warm.
- Cryogenic jacket: #A8B6C2, cold pale blue-grey, on the jacketed parts only
- Charge: violet-white #C9B6FF ramping to #FFFFFF, in the charge frames only
- WARM, not cold. The attached sprites are brown-grey with copper and brass in
  them. A cold blue-grey monochrome building is wrong and is the failure mode of
  this brief.

== RULES ==
- ADVANCED BUT STILL FACTORIO. Late tech tree: machined surfaces, sealed
  housings, flush covers, chamfered forms, indicator lamps, cryogenic jacketing,
  light used as a material, machinery implied rather than exposed.
- BUT KEEP FACTORIO'S MECHANICAL DENSITY. Match the attached sprites for how
  much stuff is on the machine: panel breaks, recessed vents, service hatches,
  grilles, bolt rings on covers, pipe and cable runs at the base, greebles in
  the shadows, honest wear. It is manufactured hardware, not a smooth prop.
- The failure to avoid is a blank seamless shell. A previous round came back
  half as colourful and much smoother than vanilla and read as generic science
  fiction. Busy and warm and machined beats clean and cold and empty.
- Not tier zero either: NO field rivets everywhere, no heavy rust sheets, no
  exposed gears, racks or linkages. Fasteners exist but are neat and purposeful.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: massive and low. Nothing is delicate.
- Nothing burns here. No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, anywhere.
- Nothing glows at rest. Violet appears only in the charge frames.
- The building sits inside a 9x9 tile square with no part crossing the edge.
- Must read as four emitters on one machine, not four separate towers or
  antennae, and not a rocket gantry. Nothing tapers to a point.

- A row of five charge key frames labelled 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%, showing

- A layer breakdown row: shadow, bare metal, charge overlay, discharge
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Panel labels only; no other text.

# Option E — the Crust Anchor

**One line.** Not a machine on the ground but a machine driven *into* it — six
legs bolted through the crust around a single electrode sunk out of sight.

## The idea

Every other option sits on the planet. This one bites it. Six massive legs splay
out from a compact head and are pinned into the rock, bracing against the
recoil of the discharge, and the working part of the machine is underground
where you cannot see it. What is visible is the anchoring.

It is the only option that treats the Core as the thing being operated on rather
than as a place to stand.

## Why it suits the Core

Gravity 50 and a metallic crust: the reaction to firing this thing has to go
somewhere, and legs pinned into rock is the honest answer. It also matches what
`control.lua` actually says — the Array delivers nothing, it fires a current
*into the crust*. This is the only design where the crust is part of the
composition.

The Core's whole surface is exposed iron-nickel rock, so a building that
half-belongs to the ground has material to work with.

## Massing and palette

A compact hexagonal head, low and thick, with six tapering legs reaching to the
edge of the footprint and ending in driven anchor plates. Rock heaved up around
each anchor. Dark iron, almost black in the shadows, with the crust's own
grey-brown around it. The least painted, most geological of the five.

## The charge display

**Down the legs, and into the ground.** Charge runs as violet from the head
outward along all six legs simultaneously, reaching the anchor plates at 100%,
and the fractures in the rock around each anchor light from within as it
arrives. The display is radial and symmetric, so it reads from any approach.

The ground lighting up is unique to this option — no other design can put the
charge outside the building's own silhouette, and it makes the machine look like
it is doing something to the planet rather than to itself.

## Plates

`base`, `charge-overlay` (legs and rock fractures lit), `shadow`, plus a ground
decal for the fractures that has to sit *under* the building. Four, and the
decal is a plate type this mod has not built before.

## Cost and risk

A fresh design and the most technically awkward: the fracture decal needs to
draw below the entity and align with legs whose positions come out of a
generated image. Also the smallest visible machine of the five — most of it is
notionally underground, which is either the strongest idea here or an excuse for
there being less building.

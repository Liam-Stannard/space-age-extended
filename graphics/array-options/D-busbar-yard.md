# Option D — the Busbar Yard

**One line.** No building at all — an open switchyard of capacitor banks and
copper busbars bolted to a plate, flat enough to see every part of.

## The idea

The other four options are objects. This one is *infrastructure*: rows of
capacitor cans, disconnect switches, and heavy copper bus running between them,
laid out on an anchored deck with walkways. It is the least dramatic and the
most factory-like, and it is the only option that looks like something the
player built rather than something they found.

Flat also means the 9 × 9 problem disappears. Nothing is tall, so nothing gets
scaled down, and the footprint is filled edge to edge.

## Why it suits the Core

Dumping 50 MW into a crust is a switchgear problem before it is anything else,
and the Core has no weather to protect the gear from — no rain, no atmosphere,
so an open yard is *correct* here in a way it would be absurd on Nauvis. The
building's honesty is the point: this is what a machine that stores a colossal
charge actually looks like.

## Massing and palette

Flat, orthogonal, dense. Rows of upright capacitor cylinders in steel frames,
copper bus overhead on short standoffs, cable trenches, a walkway grid. No
silhouette to speak of — the interest is in the density and the repetition.
Copper, grey steel, kamacite plate, rust streaks.

## The charge display

**Rows filling across the deck, like a bar.** The capacitor cans light violet
row by row from one side of the yard to the other. Because the yard is flat and
regular, the boundary between charged and uncharged rows is a clean line the eye
reads instantly, and the display is the largest of any option — it is the whole
building.

At 100% every can is lit and the bus between them glows.

## Plates

`base`, `charge-overlay` (all cans lit, masked by a moving boundary), `shadow`.
Three, and the mask is a straight edge — the simplest overlay of the five.

## Cost and risk

Cheap to draw and cheap to light. The risk is that it does not feel like an
ending. It is the least monumental option by a distance, and the last building
in a mod arguably has to be an event. There is also no bore, so the rocket rises
out of the middle of a flat yard with nothing to rise from.

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: steeply down from above, MOSTLY ROOF
with only a shallow near face. Square to the tile grid. Not rotated corner-on.
Not a flat front elevation.
The four attached sprites are style references only: match the camera, rendering,
finish and level of detail; do NOT copy the design, shape, colours or components.

== BUILDING ==
Ignition Array - option D, Busbar Yard.
An open electrical switchyard bolted to a heavy anchored deck, with
no roof and no walls. 9x9 tiles.

== FORM ==
- Completely flat and orthogonal, filling the deck edge to edge
- Dense parallel rows of upright capacitor cylinders in steel frames
- Thick copper busbars carried overhead on short ceramic standoffs
- Disconnect switches at the row ends
- Cable trenches running under a grated walkway between the rows
- Nothing rises more than a fraction of the deck's width

== COLOUR ==
- Body casing: #4A463F to #6E685C, warm low-value iron-nickel, old dark cast iron
- Pale nickel-white: #B9B4A8, confined to bolt rings and end caps
- Ceramic: #C9C0AC, the lightest value on the building
- Copper: #8A5A32, confined to busbars and straps
- Charge: violet-white #C9B6FF ramping to #FFFFFF, in the charge frames only
- NOT pale galvanised or bleached weathered steel. That is the default drift and
  it must be argued down. Warm dark desaturated steel, so violet is the only
  saturated colour on the sheet.

== RULES ==
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: squat, thick, anchored. Nothing cantilevers,
  nothing is delicate, nothing is slender.
- Nothing burns here. No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, anywhere
  on the sheet, in any panel.
- Superconducting, therefore cold. Frost and rime are correct; glowing hot metal
  is wrong.
- Nothing glows at rest. Violet appears only in the charge frames.
- The building sits inside a 9x9 tile square with no part crossing the edge.
- Must read as an electrical switchyard, not a solar farm, a battery of storage
  tanks, or a row of chemical reactors.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the building
- A tile-grid panel from directly overhead on a 9x9 tile grid, the building
  inside the grid with no part crossing the edge
- Three close-up detail panels: one upright capacitor cylinder in its steel frame; a copper busbar on its
ceramic standoffs; a cable trench crossing under the grated walkway
- A row of five charge key frames labelled 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%, showing
  the rows of capacitor cylinders lighting violet row by row across the
yard, a clean line sweeping from one side to the other, every can lit at 100%
- A layer breakdown row: shadow, bare metal, charge overlay, discharge
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Panel labels only; no other text.
```

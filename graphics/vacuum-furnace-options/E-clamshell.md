# Option E — the Clamshell

**One line.** Two heavy half-shells drawn together by a row of external draw bolts down a raised centre seam, with the sight port set in the seam itself.

## The idea

Make the seal the silhouette. The body is a single low shell running front to
back across the footprint — but it is built in **two halves**, split along the
centre line, and pulled together by a row of eight external **draw bolts** down a
raised **seam rib** on the roof. The rib is thick, machined and pale against the
dark shells, and it is the highest thing on the building.

The sight port sits in the seam rib at the near end. The fault lamp is a white
lens at the far end of the same rib. Radiator loops wrap both outer flanks.

The read is *over-clamped*: a machine that has been bolted shut harder than it
needs to be, because whoever built it did not trust a joint on a world where a
leak means losing the batch.

## Why it suits this machine

**It puts the building's argument on the roof.** §4 says the camera shows a great
deal of roof and the roof has to carry the read from above. A pale bolted spine
down the middle of a dark shell is legible from directly overhead, at any zoom,
and it is the only feature in this set that is *designed* for that view rather
than tolerated in it.

**It is a spine, and nothing else on the floor has one.** The Dross Classifier is
a stepped box, the Drop Crusher a block; both are undifferentiated from above. A
dark loaf with a bright ridge separates from either instantly.

**And the seam gives the sight port somewhere honest to be.** A port in a shell
is a hole in a wall; a port in the seam rib is a port at the one place the machine
is *deliberately* penetrated, which is the sort of detail that makes a design look
thought about rather than decorated.

## What it risks

**It argues directly with §3.2, and that has to be a decision rather than an
oversight.** The spec says the drum is *"deliberately without panel lines — it is
one piece, because a seam is a leak."* This design is two pieces with the seam
made into the feature. The counter-argument is that a seam that is visibly and
massively clamped says *sealed* more loudly than a shell with no seam says it at
all — an absence cannot be seen, and a row of draw bolts can. But it is a real
departure from the spec's stated reasoning and it should be weighed as one, not
waved through.

**A split shell invites being drawn open.** A generator asked for a clamshell will
eventually draw it hinged, ajar, or mid-opening, and an open furnace on this
building is the single worst failure available. The prompt says SHUT three times.

**A loaf with a ridge is a bread bin, a toolbox or a pipe run**, and at map zoom
the ridge might be the only thing distinguishing it from any other low grey box.
The silhouette panel is the test.

**The seam is directional**, front to back, and the template records that a
directional form invites a corner-on drawing. The prompt keeps the walls upright
and pins the seam parallel to the side faces.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Vacuum Furnace -- option E, the Clamshell.
A sealed three-by-three furnace built as two half-shells bolted together down a
raised centre seam, with the clamped seam as its signature feature.

== FORM ==
- A low heavy BODY filling the 3x3 footprint, about two thirds of a tile tall,
  its roof gently curved -- built as TWO HALF-SHELLS split along the centre line,
  which runs FRONT TO BACK, parallel to the side faces
- Down that centre line, a raised SEAM RIB: a thick machined pale rib standing
  proud of both shells, the highest thing on the building, running the full depth
  of the footprint
- Along the rib, a row of EIGHT external DRAW BOLTS with heavy nuts and washer
  plates, visibly pulling the two shells together. This is the signature feature
  and it must be the first thing the eye finds from directly above.
- The shells themselves are welded, dark, with fine seams and no panel lines
- RADIATOR LOOPS wrapping both outer flanks: cast finned tube in bolted straps
- Set into the SEAM RIB at the NEAR end: one small round SIGHT PORT, deep-set in
  a thick boss, the only opening light comes out of
- A small round white LAMP LENS at the FAR end of the same rib, so the two lights
  are never seen together
- Right face, centre: a small frost-collared FLUX FLANGE, low and unremarkable,
  running down to ground level
- A bolted skirt where the shells meet the ground, with lifting lugs at the
  corners

== COLOUR ==
- Shells and skirt: #3E3B36 to #5E584E, dark warm iron-nickel, welded -- DARKER
  than the mod's usual chassis
- Seam rib, draw bolts and machined furniture: #8A8580, pale grey -- the bright
  spine on a dark body
- Radiator loops: #6E685C
- Weld seams: #7A7268, fine lines on the shells
- Sight port glow: #E8A24A to #FFD9A0, the port in the seam rib and nothing else
- Fault lamp lens: #FFFFFF, painted white
- Flux inlet frost: #BFD8E8, small, at the flux flange only
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs, bolt
  threads and washers, flange faces and radiator straps. This is what keeps the
  building warm. Do not ration it.
- A DARK body with a PALE spine and one warm dot in it. The body must not drift
  pale or blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0-1, the landing-day machines, but deliberately built. Mostly mechanical: welded shells with visible seams, cast housings, bolted plate, heavy external fasteners, honest wear. Machined enough to look built rather than knocked together -- a properly machined seam rib and a real status lamp -- but nothing seamless, no cryogenic jacketing, no chamfered composite shells, and no light used as a material beyond the two lamps named below.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, powder, fines, grit, debris or product
  on the ground, at ports, at the seam, in trays, or spilling from the machine.
  Factorio machines never show what they make, so every port and opening is drawn
  as EMPTY machinery. This machine's whole argument is that it does not lose
  powder; a grain of it anywhere on the sheet contradicts the building.
- THE READ IS SEALED, and the seam is what seals it. The two shells are BOLTED
  SHUT in every panel. Never hinged, never ajar, never mid-opening, never with a
  gap between them, and never with anything visible in the joint. No door, no
  throat, no mouth, no chimney, no vent stack, no visible fire.
- THE SEAM RUNS FRONT TO BACK, parallel to the side faces, and the shell walls
  stay upright. Do not rotate the machine to show the seam off; the square base
  must read as a SQUARE.
- NO output port and no output chute aimed at a tile: this is a furnace, it holds
  its products until an inserter takes them, and the inserter may stand on any
  adjacent tile. Vanilla's furnaces draw no output port at all.
- EXACTLY ONE FLUID CONNECTION: the flux inlet on the RIGHT face, centre. It runs
  down to GROUND LEVEL and stops flush at the middle of that tile edge, square to
  the face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal. It does not leave the roof, it does not
  stop in mid-air and it does not cross the edge of the footprint. The flange is
  drawn into the building's own art. Keep it SMALL AND UNREMARKABLE: this machine
  runs with nothing plumbed to it half the time.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 3x3 footprint, pipework and radiator loops
  included. Height above it is fine; width is not. No visible electrical
  connector -- power arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, anywhere.
  There is no air to carry the heat away, which is what the radiators are for.
- THE HEAT BUDGET IS ONE CIRCLE. The sight port in the seam rib is the only warm
  light on this building. NOTHING GLOWS IN THE SEAM ITSELF -- a glowing joint
  would say the machine is leaking, which is the opposite of what it is for.
  Copper and brass are a MATERIAL and never glow.
- THE FAULT LAMP IS PAINTED WHITE. It is white in the hero view, the top-down
  view, the detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game.
  It is drawn coloured ONLY inside the three-state row, and only to show what
  those states look like.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as an open-fronted electric furnace -- no
  open mouth, no glowing throat, no visible interior, no fire. It must NOT read
  as a clamshell bucket, a hinged case, a toolbox, a bread bin or a plain
  shipping container: the draw bolts, the radiator loops and the sight port are
  what make it a machine, and the shells are bolted, not hinged. And it must not
  read as a pipe run or a duct.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: three of the draw bolts on the seam rib, with washer plates and the shells pulled tight against each other; and the near end of the rib with its deep-set sight port
- A THREE-STATE ROW of the same view, three frames: RUNNING with the sight port orange and the lamp dark; BLOCKED with the sight port dark and the lamp glowing amber; IDLE with both dark and the machine cold
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet VACUUM FURNACE -- CLAMSHELL -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

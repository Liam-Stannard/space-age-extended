# Option C — the Bell

**One line.** A heavy tapering bell standing over a fixed hearth base, clamped down by a ring at the joint: the state is the seal, not the light.

## The idea

A bell furnace. The lower third of the building is a fixed **hearth base** — a
squat bolted plinth that fills the footprint and never moves. Standing on it,
covering the whole charge, is a heavy **bell**: a tapering welded shell with four
lifting lugs at its crown, drawn down onto the base by a **clamp ring** at the
joint between them. Radiator loops wrap the bell's shoulder.

The sight port is set into the clamp ring, low, where bell meets base. The fault
lamp is a white lens in the crown collar at the top, as far from it as the
building goes.

The read is a machine that has been **shut**: the clamp ring is a physical seal
you can see being made, and the whole silhouette is one heavy object pressed down
onto another.

## Why it suits this machine

**It makes "sealed" a mechanism rather than an absence.** Options A and B say
*sealed* by having no openings. This one says it by showing the thing that does
the sealing — a clamp ring with visible draw dogs round a joint. That is a
positive statement of the design concept, and it is the only option that makes
one.

**A tapering shell on a square base is a silhouette nothing else on the floor
has.** Every other Core machine so far is a box, a drum or a slab. A bell reads
as a bell at map zoom and is not confusable with any of them.

**The clamp ring is a legitimate third state cue.** §3.2 gives the building one
lit state and one lamp; here the *joint* is a permanently visible feature at the
exact place a player would look for "is this shut?". It costs no light budget.

## What it risks

**A tapering shell is a hopper, a cone crusher, or a bell tent.** Turned upside
down in the reader's head it becomes a funnel — an *open* thing — which is the
worst possible misread for a machine whose whole point is that it is closed. The
crown must be plainly a solid domed cap with lugs on it, never an opening, and
the prompt says so twice.

**The taper is directional, and directional forms get turned.** The template
records the Dross Classifier coming back corner-on because a generator wants to
show a slope off. A cone is a slope in every direction at once, which is either
immunity or an invitation; the prompt keeps the base square, keeps the walls of
the base upright and puts the taper only in the bell.

**It loses §3.2's roof hatch.** The bell *is* the hatch, in the sense that lifting
it is how the charge gets in — but there is no clamped circular lid on the roof,
and the fault lamp moves to the crown collar. That is a deliberate departure and
should be weighed as one.

**A gantry would be the obvious thing to draw, and it must not be drawn.**
Anything that lifts the bell would stand outside the 3 x 3, and sideways overhang
is what makes a row of machines interleave. Lifting lugs only.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Vacuum Furnace -- option C, the Bell.
A sealed three-by-three bell furnace: a heavy tapering bell clamped down onto a
fixed hearth base, with the seal itself as the signature feature.

== FORM ==
- A fixed HEARTH BASE filling the 3x3 footprint: a squat bolted plinth about a
  third of a tile tall, SQUARE, with upright walls and chamfered corners. It
  never moves and it never tapers.
- Standing on it: a heavy welded BELL covering the whole charge -- a tapering
  shell, wider at the bottom than the top, about twice the base's height. Fine
  weld seams, no panel lines.
- At the joint between bell and base: a heavy CLAMP RING with a row of draw dogs
  round it, visibly pulling the bell down onto the base. This is the signature
  feature and it must be the first thing the eye finds.
- The bell's CROWN is a solid domed cap with FOUR lifting lugs on it. It is
  closed metal, never an opening and never a funnel mouth. No gantry, no crane,
  no rail -- lugs only.
- RADIATOR LOOPS wrapping the bell's shoulder: cast finned tube in bolted straps
- Set into the CLAMP RING, low, on the near face: one small round SIGHT PORT,
  deep-set in a thick boss, the only opening light comes out of
- A small round white LAMP LENS in the CROWN COLLAR at the top of the bell, so
  the two lights are never seen together
- Right face, centre: a small frost-collared FLUX FLANGE entering the BASE, low
  and unremarkable, running down to ground level

== COLOUR ==
- Bell and base: #3E3B36 to #5E584E, dark warm iron-nickel, welded -- DARKER
  than the mod's usual chassis
- Clamp ring, draw dogs and machined furniture: #8A8580, pale grey
- Radiator loops: #6E685C
- Weld seams: #7A7268, fine lines on the bell
- Sight port glow: #E8A24A to #FFD9A0, the port in the clamp ring and nothing
  else
- Fault lamp lens: #FFFFFF, painted white
- Flux inlet frost: #BFD8E8, small, at the flux flange only
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs,
  draw-dog threads, lug bosses, flange faces and radiator straps. This is what
  keeps the building warm. Do not ration it.
- A DARK building with one warm dot in it. The body must not drift pale or blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0-1, the landing-day machines, but deliberately built. Mostly mechanical: welded shells with visible seams, cast housings, bolted plate, honest wear. Machined enough to look built rather than knocked together -- a properly machined clamp ring and a real status lamp -- but nothing seamless, no cryogenic jacketing, no chamfered composite shells, and no light used as a material beyond the two lamps named below.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, powder, fines, grit, debris or product
  on the ground, at ports, at the joint, in trays, or spilling from the machine.
  Factorio machines never show what they make, so every port and opening is drawn
  as EMPTY machinery. This machine's whole argument is that it does not lose
  powder; a grain of it anywhere on the sheet contradicts the building.
- THE READ IS SEALED. No door, no throat, no mouth, no chimney, no vent stack, no
  visible fire, and nothing open anywhere. The bell is DOWN and the clamp ring is
  CLOSED in every panel.
- THE TAPER IS IN THE BELL ONLY. The base stays square with upright walls, and
  the square base must read as a SQUARE. Do not rotate the machine to show the
  taper off.
- NO output port and no output chute aimed at a tile: this is a furnace, it holds
  its products until an inserter takes them, and the inserter may stand on any
  adjacent tile. Vanilla's furnaces draw no output port at all.
- EXACTLY ONE FLUID CONNECTION: the flux inlet on the RIGHT face, centre, entering
  the base. It runs down to GROUND LEVEL and stops flush at the middle of that
  tile edge, square to the face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal. It does not leave
  the roof, it does not stop in mid-air and it does not cross the edge of the
  footprint. The flange is drawn into the building's own art. Keep it SMALL AND
  UNREMARKABLE: this machine runs with nothing plumbed to it half the time.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 3x3 footprint, pipework included, and NO
  GANTRY, CRANE, HOIST OR RAIL is drawn -- anything that lifted the bell would
  stand outside the footprint. Height above it is fine; width is not. No visible
  electrical connector -- power arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, anywhere.
  There is no air to carry the heat away, which is what the radiators are for.
- THE HEAT BUDGET IS ONE CIRCLE. The sight port in the clamp ring is the only
  warm light on this building. If orange appears anywhere else -- and especially
  at the bell-to-base joint -- the seal is a lie. Copper and brass are a MATERIAL
  and never glow.
- THE FAULT LAMP IS PAINTED WHITE. It is white in the hero view, the top-down
  view, the detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game.
  It is drawn coloured ONLY inside the three-state row, and only to show what
  those states look like.
- The two lights are never adjacent: the sight port is at the base joint, the
  lamp is at the crown.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as an open-fronted electric furnace -- no
  open mouth, no glowing throat, no visible interior, no fire. It must NOT read
  as a hopper, a funnel, a cone crusher, a silo or a tent: the crown is a solid
  domed cap with lifting lugs, the widest part is at the bottom, and there is no
  opening at the top at all. And it must not read as a rocket nose cone or
  anything tapering to a point.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: the clamp ring and its draw dogs at the bell-to-base joint, with the deep-set sight port in it; and the bell's crown cap with its four lifting lugs and the white lamp lens in the collar
- A THREE-STATE ROW of the same view, three frames: RUNNING with the sight port orange and the lamp dark; BLOCKED with the sight port dark and the lamp glowing amber; IDLE with both dark and the machine cold
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet VACUUM FURNACE -- BELL -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

## Round 1 — what came back

`graphics/entity/vacuum-furnace/concept/options/C-sheet.png`, generated 2026-09-09 from the prompt above with the four attachments named
in the README. One generation, no refinements.

**Measured:** luminance **63.2**, saturation **0.235**, edge density **0.122**.
Band from the four vanilla references: luminance 63.2-90.4, saturation 0.230-0.490, edge density 0.144-0.261. The *aspect* column and its "perspective low" verdict are ignored, as they are on every sheet in this repo: they measure the panel bounds, not the building.

**What landed.** The taper stayed in the bell and the base stayed square — the
failure this design was most exposed to — and the clamp ring with its draw dogs
reads as the first thing on the machine. Inside vanilla's saturation band.

**What to weigh.** The crown came back as a solid capped dome with lugs rather
than the funnel mouth the page feared, which is the whole anti-read cleared. It
is the tallest of the five, so it will lean into frame more than its neighbours.

# Option B — the Retort Bank

**One line.** Three sealed tubes lying side by side in a heavy cradle, capped with dogged plugs at the near end and joined by one common radiator manifold at the far end.

## The idea

Stop making it a pot. Three narrow **retort tubes** lie horizontally across the
footprint, front to back, side by side in a bolted cradle: each about two thirds
of a tile across, each closed at the near end by its own small dogged **plug**,
all three running into a single **radiator manifold** that spans the far end of
the machine. The manifold is the heaviest thing on the building — a cast header
with fin banks standing off it — and the tubes are what the heat comes from.

The sight port is in the centre tube's plug, dead in the middle of the near face.
The fault lamp is a white lens on the cradle rail above the manifold, as far from
the port as the footprint allows.

## Why it suits this machine

**It is a batch machine, drawn as batches.** A furnace with `source_inventory_size = 1`
runs one charge at a time, and three sealed tubes is what a sealed batch process
actually looks like — a retort bank is the real industrial answer to "heat a
powder without letting the atmosphere at it", which is this building's entire
argument.

**Its silhouette is ribbed rather than blocky.** Three parallel cylinders in a
cradle, seen as a flat black shape, is nothing like the Dross Classifier's
stepped box or the Drop Crusher's block, and nothing like the Whisker Comber's
standing drum. That is the constraint doing the most work in this set, and this
option answers it with the cheapest possible change.

**The radiator manifold earns its place.** §3.2 asks for radiator loops because
the heat has nowhere else to go on a world with no air. Here they are not a
decoration on two flanks; they are a third of the machine, at one end, plumbed to
all three tubes.

## What it risks

**Three tubes side by side is a gun battery, an organ, or a rack of gas
cylinders.** The last one is the dangerous read: cylinders in a cradle is exactly
what a bottle store looks like, and a bottle store is not a machine. The
defences — the dogged plugs, the cradle's bolted clamps, the manifold's fin banks
and the single sight port — all have to be prominent, and the tubes must be
plainly *built into* the machine rather than resting in it.

**It loses §3.2's roof hatch, and with it the lamp's home.** The spec wants a
heavy clamped hatch on the roof and the fault lamp on its rim; this design has
three small plugs on the near face instead, so the lamp moves to the cradle rail.
That is a deliberate departure and it should be weighed as one: the hatch was the
feature §4 said had to carry the read from above, and here the roof is three
curved shells and a manifold.

**The roof is round, and round surfaces measure flat.** The Whisker Comber's
standing drum came back at edge density 0.114 against vanilla's 0.144 floor for
exactly this reason. Three of them is three times the smooth area, and the fix is
the same one vanilla uses: cover them in hardware — bands, cleats, clamps,
instrument bosses.

- A DARK building with one warm dot in it. The body must not drift pale or blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0-1, the landing-day machines, but deliberately built. Mostly mechanical: welded shells with visible seams, cast housings, bolted plate, honest wear. Machined enough to look built rather than knocked together -- properly machined plugs and a real status lamp -- but nothing seamless, no cryogenic jacketing, no chamfered composite shells, and no light used as a material beyond the two lamps named below.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, powder, fines, grit, debris or product
  on the ground, at ports, at plugs, in trays, or spilling from the machine.
  Factorio machines never show what they make, so every port, plug and opening is
  drawn as EMPTY machinery. This machine's whole argument is that it does not
  lose powder; a grain of it anywhere on the sheet contradicts the building.
- THE READ IS SEALED. No door, no throat, no mouth, no chimney, no vent stack, no
  visible fire, and nothing open anywhere. The three plugs are the only openings
  in the building and all three are SHUT.
- NO output port and no output chute aimed at a tile: this is a furnace, it holds
  its products until an inserter takes them, and the inserter may stand on any
  adjacent tile. Vanilla's furnaces draw no output port at all. The three plugs
  are closures, not outputs, and nothing is aimed at a tile.
- EXACTLY ONE FLUID CONNECTION: the flux inlet on the RIGHT face, centre. It runs
  down to GROUND LEVEL and stops flush at the middle of that tile edge, square to
  the face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal. It does not leave the roof, it does not
  stop in mid-air and it does not cross the edge of the footprint. The flange is
  drawn into the building's own art. Keep it SMALL AND UNREMARKABLE: this machine
  runs with nothing plumbed to it half the time.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 3x3 footprint, pipework and manifold
  included. Height above it is fine; width is not. No visible electrical
  connector -- power arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, anywhere.
  There is no air to carry the heat away, which is what the manifold is for.
- THE HEAT BUDGET IS ONE CIRCLE. The centre plug's sight port is the only warm
  light on this building -- the other two plugs have no port. If orange appears
  anywhere else, the seal is a lie. Copper and brass are a MATERIAL and never
  glow.
- THE FAULT LAMP IS PAINTED WHITE. It is white in the hero view, the top-down
  view, the detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game.
  It is drawn coloured ONLY inside the three-state row, and only to show what
  those states look like.
- The two lights are never adjacent: the sight port is at the near end, the lamp
  at the far end.
- THE TUBE SHELLS ARE DENSE. A smooth cylinder gives the eye nothing to hold, so
  cover them in hardware: band clamps, cleats, bolt rings, instrument bosses.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as an open-fronted electric furnace -- no
  open mouth, no glowing throat, no visible interior, no fire. It must NOT read
  as a rack of gas cylinders, a bottle store, a torpedo rack or a gun battery:
  the tubes are welded into a bolted cradle with clamped saddles, they are closed
  by machined dogged plugs, and one of them carries a sight port. And it must not
  read as a set of pipes or a manifold run.

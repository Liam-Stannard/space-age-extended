# Option A — the Pot

**One line.** A squat welded drum with one big clamped hatch on its roof, radial dogs round the rim, and radiator loops on two flanks.

## The idea

§3.2, drawn exactly as written. A heavy welded drum fills the 3 x 3 and stands
about two thirds of a tile tall; its shell is one piece with fine weld seams and
no panel lines, because a seam is a leak. On the roof, taking up most of it, sits
a single circular **clamped hatch** with radial locking dogs round its rim and a
lifting eye at its centre. Radiator loops run along two flanks. Low on the near
face, deep-set in a thick boss, is the sight port. The fault lamp is a small
white lens in the hatch rim, on the opposite side of the roof from the port.

This is the conventional answer, carried as a fair comparison rather than as the
favourite. If none of the other four beats it, that is worth knowing.

## Why it suits this machine

**Everything §3.2 asks for is present and nothing is bent to fit.** The drum, the
hatch, the dogs, the radiators, the sight port, the lamp on the hatch rim, the
flux inlet. It is the only one of the five where the spec and the drawing agree
completely, which is worth one option out of five on its own.

**The hatch is on the roof, where the camera looks.** §4 makes that point in as
many words: 3 x 3 and squat means the camera shows a great deal of roof, and the
hatch has to carry the read from above. This design puts its largest feature
there.

**And it is unambiguously sealed.** One shut lid on a one-piece shell is the
plainest possible statement of the design concept. There is nothing to
misinterpret.

## What it risks

**It is a pot with a lid, and the Sealed Roboport is a dome, and the Whisker
Comber is a standing drum with a dogged lid.** That last collision is the sharp
one: the Comber was locked on 2026-09-08 as *"a squat vertical drum with a domed
lid on a ring of toggle dogs"*, and this option is a squat drum with a clamped
lid on a ring of radial dogs. Two of the nine Core buildings cannot both be that.
The differences — this one is much lower, this one has radiator loops, this one
has an orange port — all have to survive the silhouette panel, and if they do not,
this option is disqualified by a building that was picked first.

**A drum on a square footprint reads as a tank.** The radiator loops are most of
the defence, and loops on two flanks are the part of the design most likely to be
quietly dropped by a generator drawing a clean vessel.

**Its one lit state is a dot on a dark building.** At map zoom a single small
orange port on a very dark drum may simply not be visible, and the whole
RUNNING/IDLE distinction rests on it. The three-state row is where that gets
decided.

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Vacuum Furnace -- option A, the Pot.
A squat sealed three-by-three welded drum with one clamped hatch on its roof:
a furnace with no door, no chimney and no visible fire.

== FORM ==
- A heavy welded DRUM filling the 3x3 footprint, about two thirds of a tile
  tall, standing on a low bolted skirt. The shell is ONE PIECE with fine weld
  seams and no panel lines.
- On the roof, taking up most of it: one circular CLAMPED HATCH with a ring of
  radial locking dogs round its rim, a lifting eye at its centre, and a machined
  pale grey face. It is SHUT.
- RADIATOR LOOPS along two flanks: banks of cast finned tube in bolted frames,
  standing proud of the shell
- Low on the near face, deep-set in a thick armoured boss: one small round
  SIGHT PORT, the only opening that light comes out of
- A small round white LAMP LENS set into the HATCH RIM, on the far side of the
  roof from the sight port, so the two lights are never seen together
- Right face, centre: a small frost-collared FLUX FLANGE, low and unremarkable,
  running down to ground level
- Bolt rings, lifting lugs, a data head and cable cleats on the roof and skirt

== COLOUR ==
- Body: #3E3B36 to #5E584E, dark warm iron-nickel, welded -- DARKER than the
  mod's usual chassis
- Hatch, dogs and machined furniture: #8A8580, pale grey, on the roof
- Radiator loops: #6E685C
- Weld seams: #7A7268, fine lines across the body
- Sight port glow: #E8A24A to #FFD9A0, the port and nothing else
- Fault lamp lens: #FFFFFF, painted white
- Flux inlet frost: #BFD8E8, small, at the flux flange only
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs,
  hatch furniture, dog threads, flange faces and radiator fittings. This is what
  keeps the building warm. Do not ration it.
- A DARK building with one warm dot in it. The body must not drift pale or blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0-1, the landing-day machines, but deliberately built. Mostly mechanical: welded shells with visible seams, cast housings, bolted plate, honest wear. Machined enough to look built rather than knocked together -- a properly machined hatch and a real status lamp -- but nothing seamless, no cryogenic jacketing, no chamfered composite shells, and no light used as a material beyond the two lamps named below.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, powder, fines, grit, debris or product
  on the ground, at ports, at hatches, in trays, or spilling from the machine.
  Factorio machines never show what they make, so every port, hatch and opening
  is drawn as EMPTY machinery. This machine's whole argument is that it does not
  lose powder; a grain of it anywhere on the sheet contradicts the building.
- THE READ IS SEALED. No door, no throat, no mouth, no chimney, no vent stack, no
  visible fire, and nothing open anywhere. The hatch is the only opening in the
  building and it is SHUT.
- NO output port and no output chute aimed at a tile: this is a furnace, it holds
  its products until an inserter takes them, and the inserter may stand on any
  adjacent tile. Vanilla's furnaces draw no output port at all. The roof hatch is
  not an output and is not aimed at a tile.
- EXACTLY ONE FLUID CONNECTION: the flux inlet on the RIGHT face, centre. It runs
  down to GROUND LEVEL and stops flush at the middle of that tile edge, square to
  the face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal. It does not leave the roof, it does not
  stop in mid-air and it does not cross the edge of the footprint. The flange is
  drawn into the building's own art. Keep it SMALL AND UNREMARKABLE: this machine
  runs with nothing plumbed to it half the time, and a flange drawn as a feature
  would make an unplumbed machine look broken.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 3x3 footprint, pipework included. Height
  above it is fine; width is not. No visible electrical connector -- power
  arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, anywhere.
  There is no air to carry the heat away, which is what the radiators are for.
- THE HEAT BUDGET IS ONE CIRCLE. The sight port is the only warm light on this
  building. If orange appears anywhere else, the seal is a lie. Copper and brass
  are a MATERIAL and never glow.
- THE FAULT LAMP IS PAINTED WHITE. It is white in the hero view, the top-down
  view, the detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game.
  It is drawn coloured ONLY inside the three-state row, and only to show what
  those states look like.
- The two lights are never adjacent: the sight port is low on the near face, the
  lamp is on the roof.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as an open-fronted electric furnace -- no
  open mouth, no glowing throat, no visible interior, no fire. It must not read
  as a storage tank, a silo or a boiler: the hatch dogs, the radiator loops and
  the sight port are what make it a machine. And it must not read as a smooth
  domed lid on a standing drum -- that silhouette belongs to another of our
  buildings; this one is much lower and its lid is flat and clamped.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: the hatch rim, its radial dogs and the white lamp lens set into it; and the deep-set sight port in its armoured boss
- A THREE-STATE ROW of the same view, three frames: RUNNING with the sight port orange and the lamp dark; BLOCKED with the sight port dark and the lamp glowing amber; IDLE with both dark and the machine cold
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet VACUUM FURNACE -- POT -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

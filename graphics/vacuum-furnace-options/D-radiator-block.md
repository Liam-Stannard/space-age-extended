# Option D — the Radiator Block

**One line.** Nine tenths radiator and one tenth furnace: a block of close-packed fin banks with a small dark crucible plug in the middle of the roof.

## The idea

Draw the consequence instead of the machine. On a world with no air, a furnace
cannot lose heat by convection at all — the only way out is radiation, and a
radiator big enough to matter is enormous. So this building is almost entirely
**fin bank**: four close-packed stacks of cast fins in a bolted frame, filling
the 3 x 3 and standing about two thirds of a tile tall. Set flush into the middle
of the roof, small and dark against all that pale finning, is the actual furnace —
a **crucible plug** about a quarter of the footprint across, ringed with bolts.

The sight port is in the side of the crucible's boss. The fault lamp is on the
plug's bolt ring.

The joke is the proportion, and it is the same argument that won the Coil
Separator: a machine this big, to do a job that small.

## Why it suits this machine

**It is the only option that explains the 1.2 MW.** §3.2 asks for radiator loops
and gives the reason — the heat has nowhere to go — and then puts them on two
flanks as trim. This option takes the reason seriously and lets it size the
building. A player who never reads a tooltip can see that most of this machine is
spent getting rid of heat.

**Fins measure well.** Detail density is the metric the last round failed most
consistently: the Whisker Comber came back at 0.114 against vanilla's 0.144 floor
because a smooth shell gives the eye nothing to hold. A block of close-packed fins
is the highest edge-density shape available, and it is high *honestly* rather than
by scattering greebles.

**It is also the least confusable option in the set.** Nothing else on the Core's
floor is a fin block. Against the Dross Classifier's stepped box and the Drop
Crusher's block, a machine made of stripes separates immediately, even in
silhouette.

## What it risks

**A fin block is a heat sink, and a heat sink is not obviously a furnace.** This
is the Cold Plant's risk exactly, and that option's page recorded what happened:
*"it reads as a plant before it reads as a separator"*, at the price of the least
saturated sheet of fifteen. Here the defence is the crucible plug, and it must be
unmistakable — dark against pale fins, ringed with bolts, in the middle of the
roof where the camera looks. If the plug comes back subordinate to the fins, the
option has failed.

**It might read as an accumulator, a heat exchanger or a cooling tower** —
infrastructure rather than a machine. The bolt ring, the sight port and the lamp
are the only things saying otherwise, and they are all on the plug.

**Fins are a trap for a generator.** Asked for close-packed finning, a generator
draws either a radiator grille (which is a car part) or a comb of thin blades
(which will not survive being cut to a 192 px plate). The prompt asks for cast
fins in a bolted frame, thick enough to have a visible edge, and says the count
matters less than the weight.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Vacuum Furnace -- option D, the Radiator Block.
A sealed three-by-three furnace drawn as the radiator a furnace needs on a world
with no air, with the furnace itself reduced to one small plug in the roof.

== FORM ==
- The building is a BLOCK OF FIN BANKS filling the 3x3 footprint and standing
  about two thirds of a tile tall: four close-packed stacks of CAST FINS held in
  a heavy bolted frame, the fins thick enough to have a visible edge, running
  front to back. This is roughly nine tenths of the machine.
- Between the stacks, narrow machined channels with header pipes and cleats
- Set FLUSH INTO THE MIDDLE OF THE ROOF, small and dark against all that pale
  finning: the CRUCIBLE PLUG -- a circular machined cap about a quarter of the
  footprint across, ringed with bolts and raised on a thick boss. This is the
  actual furnace and it must be the first thing the eye finds.
- In the side of the crucible's boss: one small round SIGHT PORT, deep-set, the
  only opening light comes out of
- A small round white LAMP LENS on the crucible's BOLT RING, on the far side of
  the boss from the sight port
- Right face, centre: a small frost-collared FLUX FLANGE, low and unremarkable,
  entering the frame between two fin stacks and running down to ground level
- A heavy bolted skirt where the frame meets the ground, with lifting lugs

== COLOUR ==
- Frame, skirt and crucible plug: #3E3B36 to #5E584E, dark warm iron-nickel,
  welded -- DARKER than the mod's usual chassis
- Crucible bolt ring and machined furniture: #8A8580, pale grey
- Radiator fins: #6E685C -- the dominant surface of the building
- Weld seams: #7A7268, fine lines on the frame
- Sight port glow: #E8A24A to #FFD9A0, the port and nothing else
- Fault lamp lens: #FFFFFF, painted white
- Flux inlet frost: #BFD8E8, small, at the flux flange only
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs,
  header pipes, bolt rings, flange faces and fin-frame fittings. This is what
  keeps the building warm. Do not ration it, and it matters most on this design:
  a block of grey fins has nothing else carrying colour.
- A DARK plug on a PALE-FINNED block, with one warm dot in it. The body must not
  drift blue.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0-1, the landing-day machines, but deliberately built. Mostly mechanical: cast fins, bolted frames, welded shells with visible seams, honest wear. Machined enough to look built rather than knocked together -- a properly machined crucible plug and a real status lamp -- but nothing seamless, no cryogenic jacketing, no chamfered composite shells, and no light used as a material beyond the two lamps named below.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, powder, fines, grit, debris or product
  on the ground, at ports, at the plug, in the fin channels, or spilling from the
  machine. Factorio machines never show what they make, so every port and opening
  is drawn as EMPTY machinery. This machine's whole argument is that it does not
  lose powder; a grain of it anywhere on the sheet contradicts the building.
- THE READ IS SEALED. No door, no throat, no mouth, no chimney, no vent stack, no
  visible fire, and nothing open anywhere. The crucible plug is SHUT.
- THE FINS ARE CAST AND HEAVY, not thin blades and not an automotive grille:
  thick enough that each one has a visible edge and a shadow. Weight matters more
  than count.
- NO output port and no output chute aimed at a tile: this is a furnace, it holds
  its products until an inserter takes them, and the inserter may stand on any
  adjacent tile. Vanilla's furnaces draw no output port at all. The crucible plug
  is not an output and is not aimed at a tile.
- EXACTLY ONE FLUID CONNECTION: the flux inlet on the RIGHT face, centre. It runs
  down to GROUND LEVEL and stops flush at the middle of that tile edge, square to
  the face. Axis-aligned, never diagonal. It does not leave the roof, it does not
  stop in mid-air and it does not cross the edge of the footprint. The flange is
  drawn into the building's own art. Keep it SMALL AND UNREMARKABLE: this machine
  runs with nothing plumbed to it half the time.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 3x3 footprint, fins and pipework included.
  Height above it is fine; width is not. No visible electrical connector -- power
  arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: nothing burns and nothing blows about.
  No flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke, no fire, no dust cloud, no heat haze,
  anywhere. There is no air to carry the heat away, which is the whole reason
  this machine is mostly radiator.
- THE HEAT BUDGET IS ONE CIRCLE. The sight port on the crucible boss is the only
  warm light on this building. THE FINS DO NOT GLOW -- they are cold grey metal,
  and a glowing radiator would say this machine is losing heat to air it does not
  have. Copper and brass are a MATERIAL and never glow.
- THE FAULT LAMP IS PAINTED WHITE. It is white in the hero view, the top-down
  view, the detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game.
  It is drawn coloured ONLY inside the three-state row, and only to show what
  those states look like.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as an open-fronted electric furnace -- no
  open mouth, no glowing throat, no visible interior, no fire. It must NOT read
  as a heat sink, an accumulator, a cooling tower, a radiator bank or a piece of
  infrastructure: the crucible plug in the middle of the roof, its bolt ring and
  its sight port are what make it a furnace, and they must dominate the roof
  despite being small. And it must not read as a car radiator or a grille.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: the crucible plug on its boss, its bolt ring, the deep-set sight port in the boss side and the white lamp lens on the ring; and a cut through two fin stacks showing the cast fins in their bolted frame with a header pipe between them
- A THREE-STATE ROW of the same view, three frames: RUNNING with the sight port orange and the lamp dark; BLOCKED with the sight port dark and the lamp glowing amber; IDLE with both dark and the machine cold. The fins are the same grey in all three.
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet VACUUM FURNACE -- RADIATOR BLOCK -- use exactly
that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only; no
other text anywhere on the sheet.
```

Vanilla, seen and measured. Every machine is two to four large paint zones over a brown-grey and dark-steel base:
  oil refinery     rust-brown towers, but each unit its own tone: the cream lagged column, an olive-mottled tank, a weathered green
                   tank (bottom right), dark motors, bright bare-steel gears and linkage, brass rings on the pipes. Red 7 %: oxide.
  chemical plant   the ochre-yellow vessel (yellow 5 %, with the ochre in tan and rust), pale bare-steel pipes with bolted flanges,
                   red handwheels, a grey-steel lower base (grey steel 11 %).
  pumpjack         bare and grey steel pipework, a red motor box (red 4 %).
  assembler 3      green and olive-yellow paint (green 17 %, yellow 16 %): one machine, one paint.
  foundry (SA)     an oxide-red riveted vessel (rust 32 %), a cream steel frame, yellow fittings, blue cables.
  EM plant (SA)    dark steel and copper-brown, little else (variety 6.5).
  storage tank     one cream-tan paint (tan 57 %, variety 5.3): a single vessel, a single paint.
  Rust in all of them sits at seams, flanges, bolt heads and the feet; paint between stays its colour. Bare and grey steel
  together take 6-14 % of every plate; round 4 had 1.6 %.

Round 4 measured inside vanilla's whole-plate ranges by making every part the same brown: tan 42 %, rust 31 %, bare and grey
steel 1.6 %, three families, variety 6.9 -- the lowest of any multi-unit machine here.

Round 5 paints the units as vanilla does: lead for the vessels the solution lives in (the signature), oxide red for the scrubber,
ochre for the feed drum, weathered green for the receiver and the motors, cream for the pump house, the make-up pot and the
filter leaves, grey for the exchanger and the instrument house, copper for the vent condenser, dark steel for frames and skids,
bare steel for the pipes, red and yellow for handwheels and marking rings. Rust moves from an all-over mottle to the seams,
bolt heads, feet, chips and runs. Target: variety inside vanilla's range near the chemical plant's (12.6), four or more families.

### Why it won

**It is the design ten versions had already converged on, and the sheet proves
the convergence was sound.** The stroke is legible against the collar, the roof
carries visible mechanism where the camera actually looks, and the buttresses do
more for the silhouette than expected — the black shape is stepped and
castle-like rather than the plain drum the option page feared.

**Every hard constraint held.** No chute, no pipe, no flange, no loose material,
no glow, hatch shut, hazard banding confined to the base edge. Measured at
saturation **0.264** and edge density **0.158**, both inside vanilla's band, on a
building whose palette is grey armour, a near-black weight and one yellow stripe.

**And the lamp is painted white** in the hero view, the top-down, both details
and the silhouette, coloured only inside the three-state row — which is the
instruction most likely to be lost and the one that would have baked a permanent
amber lamp into the plate.

### What to fix in production, found on review

1. **A third of the box is still all that moves.** The animation gate stands: at
   ~33 % this is under vanilla's 72–113 % band, and it was the weakest of the
   five on that measure. It is far better than the Dross Classifier's 5.2 %, but
   stage 1 should push the crown *taller and wider* rather than let it shrink,
   and the four-frame row is where that gets checked.
2. **The plate drifts warm.** The palette strip came back browner than §3.3's
   `#4A463F`–`#6E685C`, and luminance measured 63.0 against a floor of 63.2.
   Dark is correct for this building; brown is not. Stage 1 should pull the body
   back toward neutral iron-nickel and leave the warmth to the copper.
3. **The front hatch reads as a bank-vault door.** Big round plate, radial dogs,
   central boss. It suits "sealed", but it is the single most generic element on
   the machine and it competes with the collar for attention. Smaller, or
   off-centre.
4. **Keep the lift gear.** Ten versions of history say the roof wants to come
   back as a plain lid. The gears, rack and pawl must stay a separate, brighter
   assembly.

**One line.** A tall armoured cylinder ringed by buttresses, with the forged weight's crown seated in a machined collar at the centre of its flat roof, hauled up a rack by geared drums and dropped by a trip pawl.

**What moves:** the crown and its shank, lifting clear of the collar by most of its own
height — a moving box of roughly **1.0 × 1.3 tiles, about 33 % of the building's
own width**. The lift gears turn and the pawl kicks, but they are small. This is
the weakest of the five on the animation gate, and it is the one ten previous
versions produced without anyone measuring it.

## The idea

§3.1 as it currently stands, drawn exactly as written, and carried here as the
fair comparison rather than as the favourite.

A heavy armoured cylinder with a slightly tapered wall fills the 3 × 3 and stands
about two tiles tall on a square base plate. External buttresses ring it, standing
proud of the wall and running from the base to just under the roof. The roof is
flat, and at its centre a hard machined **collar** holds the chamfered crown of
the drop weight.

The roof also carries the mechanism, which is version 10's correction and the best
thing about this design: a pair of heavy toothed gears mesh with a **vertical
rack** cut into the weight's shank, driven by a compact geared motor housing on
one side, with a stout sprung **trip pawl** that engages the rack and kicks clear
to release. That is how a board drop hammer actually behaves — wound up, then let
go — and it puts machinery on the surface the camera shows most of.

## Why it suits this machine

**The collar is a datum, and that is what makes the stroke legible.** A crown
that merely bobs inside a recess reads as nothing. A crown that lifts *clear* of a
hard horizontal ring and returns to sit flush against it gives the eye a fixed
line to measure against.

**It is unambiguously sealed and unambiguously heavy.** A thick-walled buttressed
cylinder is the plainest statement of mass available, which suits the one machine
on the planet whose whole argument is weight.

**Everything §3.1 and §3.2 ask for is present and nothing is bent to fit.** It is
the only one of the five where the spec and the drawing agree completely, which is
worth one option out of five on its own.

## What it risks

**It is a drum, and two locked buildings on this floor are already drums.** The
Vacuum Furnace is a welded drum with a clamped lid on its roof; the Whisker Comber
is a standing drum with a domed lid on a ring of dogs. This is a standing drum
with a machined collar on its roof. Three of nine cannot all be that — and this
one was designed before either of the others was locked, so it is the one that has
to move. The buttresses and the proud crown are the whole defence and they have to
survive the silhouette panel.

**A third of the box is not much motion.** Against vanilla's 3-tile crafting
machines — 72 % on `assembling-machine-3`, 101 % on the centrifuge, 113 % on the
electromagnetic plant — a crown at 33 % is well under the band. Far better than the
Dross Classifier's 5.2 %, and still the least of these five.

**The lift gear is the part a generator will quietly smooth away.** Ten versions of
history say the roof wants to come back as a plain lid.

## Concept Sheet Prompt

```text
FACTORIO SPACE AGE BUILDING -- CONCEPT SHEET

== CAMERA ==
Match the attached vanilla sprites exactly: looking steeply down from above,
MOSTLY ROOF with only a shallow near face visible. Square to the tile grid --
the near face parallel to the bottom edge of the frame, the side faces parallel
to the left and right edges. The square base must read as a SQUARE, never as a
diamond or a rhombus. Not rotated corner-on. Not a flat front elevation.
The attached vanilla sprites are STYLE references only: match the camera,
rendering, finish, LEVEL OF DETAIL and COLOUR TEMPERATURE; do NOT copy the
design, shape or components. The attached sheet of our own is a FORMAT reference
only: match its panel layout and information panels. The machine in it is a
different building of ours and this one must look like nothing else.

== BUILDING ==
Drop Crusher -- option A, the Sealed Hammer.
A sealed three-by-three impact crusher: it breaks ore by lifting a mass and
letting it fall. At fifty gravities the fall does the work a motor would have to
do anywhere else.

== FORM ==
- A tall armoured CYLINDER with a slightly tapered wall, about two tiles tall,
  filling the 3x3 and standing on a square riveted BASE PLATE
- EXTERNAL BUTTRESSES ringing the cylinder, standing proud of the wall and
  running from the base plate to just under the roof
- The roof is FLAT. At its centre, a hard machined COLLAR -- a heavy horizontal
  ring -- with the chamfered CROWN of the drop weight seated in it
- LIFT GEAR ON THE ROOF, flanking the collar: a pair of heavy toothed gears
  meshing with a VERTICAL RACK cut into the weight's shank, driven by a compact
  geared motor housing on one side. Drawn in a BRIGHTER metal than the armour so
  it reads as a separate assembly bolted on, not as more shell
- A stout sprung TRIP PAWL beside one gear, engaging the rack
- An armoured INSPECTION HATCH with radial locking dogs on the front face
- A small round white LAMP LENS on the roof, at the front edge, clear of the gear
- Riveted seams, bolt lines, lifting lugs, cable cleats, honest wear

== COLOUR ==
- Cylinder, buttresses and base: #4A463F to #6E685C, dark warm iron-nickel
- Drop weight crown: #2E2C29 body with chamfers up to #6A6660. DARK, but with a
  real value range across its chamfers so it reads as forged steel and NOT as a
  flat black hole in the picture
- Collar, gears, rack and pawl: #8A857C, deliberately brighter than the armour
- Hazard banding: #C8A23A, on the base plate edge ONLY, a stripe and not a
  decoration
- Lamp lens: #FFFFFF, painted white
- Copper and brass: #8A5A32 to #C88A4A, VISIBLE and used freely on bus runs,
  mechanism furniture, bearing caps and bolt lines. This is what keeps the
  building warm. Do not ration it.

== RULES ==
- REGISTER: TIER 0, the landing-day machines, and CRUDE ON PURPOSE. Riveted
  plate, cast housings, EXPOSED GEARS, racks, pawls and chain, weld seams,
  hazard tape, honest wear. Its visible mechanism is correct for its tier and
  must NOT be modernised: no sealed composite shells, no chamfered panels, no
  jacketing, no light used as a material.
- NOTHING ON THIS MACHINE IS HOT AND NOTHING GLOWS. No furnace light, no ember,
  no heat, no warm light anywhere. The white lamp lens is the ONLY light on the
  building. Copper and brass are a MATERIAL and never glow.
- THE LAMP LENS IS PAINTED WHITE. White in the hero view, the top-down view, the
  detail panels and the silhouette -- the engine colours it in game. It is drawn
  coloured ONLY inside the three-state row.
- Draw NO loose material anywhere: no ore, rock, powder, fines, grit, dust or
  debris on the ground, at hatches, in trays, or falling from the machine. This
  is a crusher and it must still be drawn EMPTY -- Factorio machines never show
  what they make.
- NO OUTPUT CHUTE, spout, boom, bin, tray, hopper or opening of any kind. This
  machine holds its output in an inventory until an inserter takes it, and an
  inserter may stand on any adjacent tile. Draw none at all.
- NO PIPES AND NO FLUID CONNECTION ANYWHERE. This machine has no fluid box, so a
  drawn flange would be a socket a player can never plumb. No pipe, no flange, no
  hose, no hydraulic line, no coolant run.
- THE MACHINE IS SEALED. No visible anvil bed, no open frame, no jaw plates, no
  gullet, no conveyor, no visible interior. Every hatch is SHUT.
- Nothing extends sideways past the 3x3 footprint. Height above it is fine; width
  is not. No visible electrical connector -- power arrives from a pole off-frame.
- Airless metallic world, gravity 50: no flame, no exhaust, no steam, no smoke,
  no dust cloud, nothing blowing about, anywhere.
- Not generic science fiction: no neon strip lighting, no holograms, no decals,
  no hazard chevrons used as decoration.
- FORBIDDEN READS: it must NOT read as a rotating mill or roller crusher -- that
  is a different machine on a different world. Not a jaw crusher: no opposed
  plates, no gnashing jaws. Not a silo, a tank or a storage vessel. And not a
  smooth domed lid on a plain drum -- that silhouette belongs to two other
  buildings of ours.

== PANELS ==
- A large hero view of the whole building
- A silhouette panel: the same building as a flat black shape on grey, to prove
  it is readable with no detail at all
- A top-down view from directly overhead, WITHOUT grid lines drawn on it
- Two close-up detail panels: the roof collar with the crown seated in it and the gears meshing with the rack; and the trip pawl engaged on the rack
- A FOUR-FRAME MOTION ROW of the same view: seated with the pawl engaged; gears turning and the crown climbing clear of the collar; at the top of the stroke with the crown standing proud by most of its own height and the pawl kicking clear; seated again with the collar rim unbroken and the gears freewheeling
- A THREE-STATE ROW of the same view, three frames: RUNNING with the lamp lens
  glowing GREEN; BLOCKED with the lamp glowing AMBER; IDLE with the lamp dark and
  the machine still
- A palette strip of eight colour swatches

== OUTPUT ==
One landscape concept sheet, 3:2, labelled panels on a dark charcoal background,
in the style of a game art bible page. Title the sheet DROP CRUSHER -- SEALED HAMMER -- use
exactly that name, do not invent a generic descriptive title. Panel labels only;
no other text anywhere on the sheet. No logos, wordmarks or watermarks anywhere.
```
